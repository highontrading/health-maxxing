Some health advice survives for decades on confidence alone. Here are five claims worth letting go of, and what to believe instead.

## "You need eight glasses of water a day"

There is nothing special about eight. Needs vary with your size, the weather and how active you are, and food, tea and coffee all count toward the total. For most healthy adults, thirst is a reliable guide.

Read: [How Much Water Do You Really Need?](/articles/how-much-water-do-you-need)

## "You can burn fat from one specific place"

Exercising a muscle does not strip the fat sitting on top of it. Your body decides where fat comes off, and it does so everywhere at once. Crunches build abdominal muscle; they do not choose where you lose fat.

Read: [Weight Loss That Lasts](/articles/weight-loss-that-lasts)

## "Carbs make you fat"

No single nutrient does. Whole grains, beans, fruit and vegetables are carbohydrates, and they are among the foods most consistently linked with good health. The carbs worth cutting back on are added sugars and refined starches.

Read: [Carbs, Explained](/articles/carbs-explained)

## "Antibiotics will clear up a cold"

Colds and flu are caused by viruses, and antibiotics only work on bacteria. Taking them when they cannot help exposes you to side effects and speeds up antibiotic resistance.

Read: [Antibiotics: When They Help and When They Hurt](/articles/antibiotics-when-they-help)

## "You can catch up on sleep at the weekend"

A lie-in takes the edge off, but it does not fully undo a week of short nights, and a shifting wake-up time makes Monday harder. Regular hours beat heroic recovery.

Read: [How Much Sleep Do You Need?](/articles/how-much-sleep-do-you-need)
