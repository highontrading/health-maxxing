Wearables can now track dozens of things about you. Most of them are trivia. A handful are worth your attention, and none of them needs a subscription.

## Blood pressure

Below 120/80 mm Hg is normal under US guidelines; 130/80 or higher on repeated readings counts as hypertension there, while many other countries draw the line at 140/90. One reading means little. Sit quietly for five minutes, take two, and look at the average over a few days.

Check a reading: [Blood pressure checker](/tools/blood-pressure)

## Waist-to-height ratio

Fat around your middle matters more for your heart and metabolism than fat elsewhere, and BMI cannot see it. Keep your waist under half your height.

Work it out: [Waist-to-height calculator](/tools/waist-to-height) · Read: [BMI, Waist Size and Body Fat](/articles/bmi-waist-and-body-composition)

## Resting heart rate

For most adults, 60 to 100 beats a minute is normal, and fitter people often sit lower. The single number matters less than the trend: a resting rate that climbs for several days can be an early sign of illness, poor sleep or too much training.

Read: [Resting Heart Rate and HRV](/articles/resting-heart-rate-and-hrv)

## Weekly activity minutes

Not steps, not calories burned: minutes of movement that raise your heart rate. Aim for 150 a week and count everything, including the brisk walk to the shops.

Find your zones: [Heart rate zone calculator](/tools/heart-rate-zones)

> [!NOTE] What you can ignore
> Sleep "scores", stress scores and calories burned are estimates built on estimates. Treat them as a rough direction, never a verdict. How you feel during the day is better data than a ring telling you that you slept badly.
