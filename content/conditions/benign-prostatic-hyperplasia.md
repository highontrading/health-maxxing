## What it is

The prostate is a walnut-sized gland that sits below the bladder and wraps around the urethra, the tube that carries urine out of the body. Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a non-cancerous increase in its size that can squeeze the urethra and irritate the bladder.[^1]

It is very common with age: BPH affects about half of men between 51 and 60 and up to 90% of men over 80.[^1] BPH is not cancer and does not raise your risk of [prostate cancer](/conditions/prostate-cancer), although the two can exist together and cause similar symptoms.[^2]

## Symptoms

- A weak or slow stream.
- Difficulty starting to urinate.
- A stream that stops and starts.
- Straining, and dribbling at the end.
- Feeling that your bladder has not emptied.
- Needing to urinate often or urgently.
- Waking at night to urinate.[^1][^2]

The size of the prostate does not predict how bad the symptoms are. Some men with very large prostates have few symptoms, and the reverse is also true.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

The cause is not fully understood, but it is linked to aging and to changes in male hormones.[^1] BPH is more likely if you are over 40, have a family history of it, or have obesity, type 2 diabetes, heart and circulatory disease or a low level of physical activity.[^1]

## Diagnosis

An assessment usually includes:[^1][^3]

- A symptom questionnaire, the International Prostate Symptom Score, which grades symptoms as mild, moderate or severe.
- A urine test to rule out infection and blood.
- A rectal examination, in which the doctor feels the size and texture of the prostate with a gloved finger.
- Sometimes a PSA blood test, a urine flow test, a scan to measure how much urine is left after you go, or a bladder diary.

## Treatment

Treatment depends on how much the symptoms bother you:[^3]

- **Watchful waiting**, with lifestyle changes, suits mild symptoms.[^2][^3]
- **Alpha blockers**, such as tamsulosin, relax the muscle in the prostate and the neck of the bladder, and act quickly.[^1][^3]
- **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors**, such as finasteride, shrink the prostate over several months and are used for larger glands. They can cause sexual side effects, and they roughly halve PSA readings, which matters when PSA is used to check for cancer.[^3]
- **Other medicines** include daily tadalafil and, for urgency and frequency, medicines that calm the bladder.[^3]
- **Procedures** are options when medicines are not enough or complications develop. They range from transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP), the long-established operation, and laser surgery, to less invasive techniques such as steam treatment or small implants that hold the urethra open.[^2][^4] Each involves a different trade-off between symptom relief, recovery time and sexual side effects.

Saw palmetto, a popular supplement, has not relieved urinary symptoms any better than placebo in rigorous trials.[^5]

## Prevention and self-care

There is no proven way to prevent BPH, but these steps can ease symptoms:[^1][^2]

- Drink less in the evening, and cut down on caffeine and alcohol.
- Take your time to empty your bladder, then try again a few moments later.
- Avoid constipation, stay active and keep a healthy weight.
- Ask whether any of your medicines could be contributing. Some decongestants and antihistamines make it harder to urinate.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor about urinary symptoms that bother you or are getting worse. The same symptoms can come from an infection, a bladder problem, diabetes or, less often, prostate cancer, so they are worth checking.[^2] [Prostate Health: BPH, PSA Tests and What to Watch For](/articles/prostate-health-guide) explains what to expect.

**Get emergency care if you cannot urinate at all.** Sudden, painful inability to pass urine needs a catheter to relieve it. Also seek urgent help for fever and chills with urinary symptoms, blood in your urine, or severe pain in your lower abdomen.[^1]
