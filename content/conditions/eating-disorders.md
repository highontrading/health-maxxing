## What it is

Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions marked by persistent disturbances in eating behavior and in the thoughts and feelings that go with it. They are not a lifestyle choice or a phase, and they can be life-threatening.[^1]

The main types are:[^1]

- **Anorexia nervosa:** restricting food, an intense fear of gaining weight and a distorted view of one's own body
- **Bulimia nervosa:** repeated episodes of binge eating followed by behaviors meant to compensate
- **Binge-eating disorder:** repeated episodes of eating large amounts with a feeling of loss of control, without regular compensating behaviors
- **Avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID):** limiting the amount or range of food for reasons other than body image

Eating disorders affect people of all ages, genders, ethnic backgrounds and body sizes. Someone can look healthy and be seriously ill, so you cannot tell by appearance.[^1]

## Symptoms

Early signs are often changes in behavior: preoccupation with food, weight or shape, new rigid rules about eating, avoiding meals with others, eating in secret, or exercising compulsively. Withdrawal, irritability and low mood often come with them.[^1][^3]

Physical effects depend on the disorder. They can include dizziness, fainting, feeling cold, tiredness, missed periods and digestive problems, and serious complications can affect the heart, bones and body chemistry.[^1] Anorexia nervosa has one of the highest death rates of any mental health condition, from medical complications and from suicide.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

There is no single cause, and nobody is to blame. Eating disorders arise from a mix of genetic, biological, psychological and social factors.[^1][^4] They most often begin in the teens or young adulthood, but can start in childhood or later in life.[^1]

They frequently occur alongside anxiety, depression or obsessive-compulsive disorder, and they tend to run in families.[^3]

## Diagnosis

A doctor or mental health professional asks about eating patterns, thoughts about food and body, and physical symptoms. They also check physical health with an examination, blood tests and sometimes a heart tracing (ECG).[^2]

Weight alone does not decide the diagnosis. NICE states that single measures, such as body mass index or how long someone has been ill, should not be used to decide whether to offer treatment.[^2]

## Treatment

Early treatment improves the chance of a full recovery, and recovery is possible.[^1] Care usually comes from a team and combines:[^1][^2]

- **Psychological therapy.** For children and teenagers, family-based therapy is the leading approach. For adults, options include forms of cognitive behavioral therapy designed for eating disorders. For bulimia and binge-eating disorder, NICE recommends starting with a structured program guided by a clinician.
- **Medical monitoring and nutritional support**, with day care or hospital care if physical health is at risk.
- **Medication** in a supporting role for some people. NICE advises that medication should not be the only treatment.

## Prevention and self-care

Self-help does not replace professional treatment, but the people around someone can make a real difference. If you are worried about a friend or relative, raise it privately and calmly, talk about how they seem to be feeling instead of about food or weight, avoid blame, and encourage them to see a professional.[^6]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if food, weight or body shape are taking over your thoughts, or if you notice the signs above in yourself or someone else. You do not need to be underweight, or to feel "ill enough", to deserve help.[^2] [How to Get More Out of a Doctor's Appointment](/articles/how-to-talk-to-your-doctor) can help you prepare for the conversation.

Call your local emergency number for fainting, chest pain, an irregular heartbeat, severe weakness or confusion, or vomiting blood.

If you are thinking about harming yourself or ending your life, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line straight away. Find A Helpline lists free, confidential services by country.[^5]
