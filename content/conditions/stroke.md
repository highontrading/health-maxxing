## What it is

**A stroke is a medical emergency. If you suspect one, call your local emergency number immediately.**

A stroke happens when part of the brain loses its blood supply, and brain cells begin to die within minutes.[^3] There are two main types:[^2]

- **Ischemic stroke**, the most common type (about 87% of strokes, according to the American Stroke Association): a clot blocks an artery supplying the brain.
- **Hemorrhagic stroke**: a blood vessel bursts and bleeds into or around the brain.

A transient ischemic attack (TIA, or "mini-stroke") is a temporary blockage whose symptoms pass within minutes to hours. It is a warning that a full stroke may follow and needs the same emergency response.[^2][^4]

Stroke is among the top three causes of death worldwide.[^5]

## Symptoms

Symptoms come on suddenly. Remember FAST:[^1]

- **Face:** one side droops or feels numb. Ask the person to smile.
- **Arms:** one arm is weak or numb and drifts down when both are raised.
- **Speech:** slurred, garbled or absent.
- **Time:** call emergency services at once.

Other sudden signs are numbness or weakness in a leg, confusion, trouble seeing in one or both eyes, dizziness or loss of balance, and a severe headache with no known cause.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

[High blood pressure](/conditions/high-blood-pressure) is the most important risk factor you can change. Others are smoking, diabetes, high cholesterol, [atrial fibrillation](/conditions/atrial-fibrillation), excess weight, physical inactivity, heavy drinking, older age and a previous stroke or TIA.[^3][^7]

## Diagnosis

Hospital teams move fast. A CT or MRI brain scan shows whether the stroke is caused by a clot or by bleeding, which determines the treatment.[^3][^6] Further tests, such as an ECG to look for heart problems, then help find the cause.[^3]

## Treatment

For ischemic stroke, the aim is to reopen the artery:[^6]

- Clot-dissolving medicine (thrombolysis) can be given to eligible people within 4.5 hours of the start of symptoms, and sometimes later when brain scans show tissue that can still be saved.
- Thrombectomy, in which a catheter is used to pull the clot out of a large artery, is usually done within 6 hours, and up to 24 hours in selected people.

That is why the time symptoms began matters so much. Hemorrhagic stroke is treated by controlling blood pressure, reversing any blood-thinning medicines and sometimes surgery.[^3][^4]

Afterward, care focuses on preventing another stroke, with antiplatelet or anticoagulant medicines, statins and blood pressure control, and on rehabilitation with a team of specialists, including physical therapy.[^3][^4]

## Prevention and self-care

The American Heart Association estimates that up to 80% of strokes may be preventable.[^7] The steps that matter most are keeping blood pressure in range, not smoking, staying active, eating a Mediterranean-style diet, limiting alcohol, and treating diabetes, high cholesterol and atrial fibrillation.[^3][^7] Our [blood pressure checker](/tools/blood-pressure) shows where your readings fall.

## When to see a doctor

**Any stroke symptom is an emergency, even if it fades. Call your local emergency number right away. Do not drive yourself, and do not wait to see if it improves.** Tell the crew when the symptoms started, or when the person was last seen well.[^1][^3]

Do not give aspirin for a suspected stroke. Some strokes are caused by bleeding, which aspirin could make worse, so hospitals scan the brain before giving it.[^3][^8]

After a stroke or TIA, keep your follow-up appointments and take preventive medicines as prescribed. [Heart Attack and Stroke: Warning Signs to Act On Fast](/articles/heart-attack-and-stroke-warning-signs) has more.
