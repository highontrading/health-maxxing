## What it is

The WHO defines obesity as a chronic, complex disease in which excess body fat can impair health.[^1] It is not a character flaw. Body weight is regulated by genes, hormones and brain circuits that control appetite, all interacting with the food, work and sleep environment you live in.[^3][^5]

In 2022, 1 in 8 people worldwide were living with obesity, including 890 million adults. Adult rates have more than doubled since 1990.[^1]

## Symptoms

Obesity itself may cause no symptoms. Some people notice breathlessness with activity, snoring and poor sleep, joint or back pain, or tiredness.[^4] Over time it raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, sleep apnea, osteoarthritis, fatty liver disease and several cancers.[^1][^4]

Weight stigma does harm of its own. An international commission of experts described bias and stigma as major obstacles to preventing and treating obesity.[^3]

## Causes and risk factors

There is rarely one cause. Contributors include inherited tendencies, eating patterns, low physical activity, too little sleep, long-term stress, health conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome, and the environment around you. Some medicines also promote weight gain, including certain antidepressants, antipsychotics, steroids and insulin.[^5]

Biology also resists weight loss. In one study, hormones that drive hunger were still raised, and fullness signals still lowered, a year after people lost weight by dieting. That helps explain why regain is common and is not simply a question of willpower.[^6]

## Diagnosis

The usual starting point is [body mass index (BMI)](/tools/bmi): weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. For adults, the WHO classes 25 or more as overweight and 30 or more as obesity.[^1]

BMI is a screening tool. It cannot tell fat from muscle or show where fat sits. The NHS uses lower thresholds (27.5 or more for obesity) for people with Asian, Chinese, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family backgrounds, and treats a [waist-to-height ratio](/tools/waist-to-height) of 0.5 or more as a sign of increased risk.[^4]

In 2025 a Lancet commission recommended confirming excess body fat with a second measure, such as waist size. It also proposed separating clinical obesity, where excess fat is already affecting organs or daily function, from preclinical obesity, where it is not yet.[^3] A full assessment also checks blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol and sleep.[^2]

## Treatment

Care is long term, as for any chronic condition, and is tailored to the person. The main options are:

- **Behavioral programs** covering food, activity, sleep and psychological support. Losing 5% to 10% of starting weight improves blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol.[^2]
- **Medication.** In randomized trials in adults without diabetes, weekly semaglutide produced an average weight loss of about 15% at 68 weeks, compared with about 2% on placebo, and the highest dose of tirzepatide about 21% at 72 weeks, compared with about 3%.[^7][^8] These are prescription medicines for long-term use alongside lifestyle support.
- **Metabolic (bariatric) surgery**, such as sleeve gastrectomy or gastric bypass, for people with more severe obesity or related conditions. Eligibility rules differ between countries.[^2]

[GLP-1 Drugs Explained](/articles/glp-1-weight-loss-drugs-explained) covers the medicines in detail.

## Prevention and self-care

The WHO stresses that preventing obesity is a job for societies, not only individuals: food prices, marketing and the design of towns and workplaces all shape what is easy to do.[^1]

For individuals, the basics are regular physical activity, enough sleep and an eating pattern built mostly on minimally processed foods.[^2] [Weight Loss That Lasts](/articles/weight-loss-that-lasts) looks at what the evidence supports.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if your weight is affecting your health, sleep, mobility or mood, or if you would like support. Ask for checks of your blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol.[^4]

Get advice sooner for rapid, unexplained weight gain, or for loud snoring with pauses in breathing and daytime sleepiness, which suggest [sleep apnea](/conditions/sleep-apnea). You are entitled to respectful care at any size.
