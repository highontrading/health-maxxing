## What it is

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a disorder in which the muscles of the throat relax during sleep and the airway repeatedly narrows or closes. Breathing pauses or becomes shallow, oxygen levels dip, and the brain briefly rouses you to reopen the airway.[^1] This can happen many times an hour, and because it happens during sleep, many people do not know they have it.

One analysis estimated that 936 million adults aged 30 to 69 worldwide have OSA, and that it is moderate to severe in about 425 million of them.[^3]

## Symptoms

During sleep, often noticed by a partner:[^2]

- Loud snoring.
- Breathing that stops and starts.
- Gasping, snorting or choking noises.
- Waking up a lot.

During the day:[^2]

- Feeling very tired.
- Finding it hard to concentrate.
- Mood swings.
- A headache when you wake up.

Not everyone who snores has sleep apnea.

## Causes and risk factors

Excess weight is the most important risk factor, partly because fat around the neck narrows the airway. Others include a large neck, being male, older age, a family history, large tonsils (the usual cause in children), smoking, drinking alcohol or taking sedatives before bed, and sleeping on your back.[^1][^2]

## Diagnosis

OSA is diagnosed with a sleep study. This can be done overnight in a sleep laboratory or, for adults who are likely to have moderate to severe OSA and have no major heart or lung disease, with a home sleep apnea test. Questionnaires can flag your risk, but the American Academy of Sleep Medicine says they should not be used to make the diagnosis.[^4]

Severity is graded by the apnea-hypopnea index (AHI), the number of breathing pauses and shallow breaths per hour of sleep:[^2]

| Severity | AHI (events per hour) |
| --- | --- |
| Mild | 5 to 14 |
| Moderate | 15 to 30 |
| Severe | More than 30 |

## Treatment

- **CPAP** (continuous positive airway pressure) is the main treatment for moderate to severe OSA. A bedside machine sends air through a mask to hold the airway open. Trials show that it reduces sleepiness and improves quality of life and blood pressure. They have not clearly shown that it prevents heart attacks or strokes.[^5]
- **Oral appliances**, which hold the lower jaw forward, help some people with milder OSA or who cannot use CPAP.[^1][^2]
- **Weight loss** can reduce OSA and sometimes resolve it. In a 2024 randomized trial, the weight-loss medicine tirzepatide cut breathing pauses by about half or more in adults with obesity and moderate to severe OSA.[^6]
- **Surgery or an implanted nerve stimulator** is an option for selected people, and removing enlarged tonsils is a common treatment in children.[^1]

## Prevention and self-care

Losing excess weight, cutting down on alcohol (especially in the evening), not smoking and sleeping on your side all help.[^2] They support treatment but rarely replace it in moderate or severe OSA. If you have CPAP, use it every night, and ask for help with the mask if it is uncomfortable. See [Weight Loss That Lasts](/articles/weight-loss-that-lasts) for what works.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you snore loudly and feel tired during the day, or if someone has seen you stop breathing or gasp in your sleep.[^2] Untreated OSA is linked to [high blood pressure](/conditions/high-blood-pressure), heart disease, stroke and type 2 diabetes, and to accidents caused by tiredness.[^1][^2]

Do not drive when you feel sleepy. In some countries, including the UK, you may have to tell the driver licensing authority about sleep apnea.[^2]
