## What it is

Allergic rhinitis is inflammation of the inside of the nose caused by an allergic reaction to particles in the air, such as pollen, house dust mites, mold spores or flakes of animal skin.[^1] When pollen is the trigger and symptoms are seasonal, it is usually called hay fever. It is very common, affecting about 1 in 5 people in the UK.[^1]

It is not an infection and cannot be passed on. It is more than a nuisance, though: poorly controlled symptoms disrupt sleep, concentration, work and school.[^1]

## Symptoms

Typical symptoms are sneezing, an itchy, runny or blocked nose, and itchy, red, watery eyes. Many people also have an itchy throat, mouth or ears, a cough, a reduced sense of smell, headache and tiredness.[^2]

Itching is the giveaway, along with timing: hay fever can last for weeks or months, while a cold usually clears in one to two weeks.[^2][^5]

## Causes and risk factors

In allergic rhinitis the immune system treats a harmless substance as a threat and releases chemicals, including histamine, that inflame the lining of the nose.[^1] Common triggers are:[^1][^4]

- tree, grass and weed pollens, which peak at different times of year
- house dust mites
- animal dander: flakes of skin, saliva and urine
- mold spores

You are more likely to have it if allergies run in your family or if you have asthma or eczema.[^1] The link with [asthma](/conditions/asthma) works both ways: many people have both, and treating the nose is part of keeping the chest under control.[^3]

## Diagnosis

Doctors usually diagnose allergic rhinitis from your symptoms and when they occur. If the trigger is unclear or treatment is not working, a skin prick test or a blood test for allergy antibodies can identify what you react to.[^1][^3]

## Treatment

Treatment is stepped up according to how troublesome the symptoms are:[^2][^3]

- **Steroid nasal sprays** are the most effective single treatment for persistent nasal symptoms. They work best used every day and can take several days to reach full effect.
- **Non-sedating antihistamine tablets,** such as cetirizine, loratadine or fexofenadine, help sneezing and itching. Older, sedating antihistamines are not recommended.
- **Antihistamine nasal sprays and eye drops** act quickly on the nose and eyes.
- **Decongestant nasal sprays** are for a few days only, because longer use can make congestion worse.
- **Allergen immunotherapy** is for symptoms that persist despite medicines. Regular injections or tablets that dissolve under the tongue, taken over several years, retrain the immune response.

Many of these are sold without a prescription, to be used as directed on the label. A pharmacist can help you choose. [Seasonal Allergies: A Relief Plan That Works](/articles/seasonal-allergies-relief) covers spray technique and timing.

## Prevention and self-care

You cannot prevent the allergy itself, but you can lower your exposure.[^2][^4]

- **Pollen:** check the pollen forecast, keep windows closed when counts are high, shower and change clothes after being outdoors, wear wraparound sunglasses, and do not dry laundry outside on high-pollen days.
- **Dust mites and pets:** wash bedding regularly in hot water, use allergen-proof covers on mattresses and pillows, and keep pets out of the bedroom.

Rinsing the nose with salt water can wash out allergens and mucus.[^1] Make the solution with distilled, sterile or previously boiled and cooled water, never water straight from the tap, because of a rare risk of serious infection.[^6]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if pharmacy treatments are not controlling your symptoms, if symptoms are getting worse, or if they disturb your sleep, work or school.[^1][^2] Also get checked if only one side of your nose is blocked, or you have nosebleeds or facial pain, since these suggest another cause.

Wheezing, chest tightness or shortness of breath may mean asthma and needs assessment.[^4] Severe breathing difficulty is an emergency: call your local emergency number.
