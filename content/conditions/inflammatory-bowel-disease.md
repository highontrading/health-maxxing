## What it is

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is the name for conditions that cause long-term inflammation of the digestive tract. There are two main types.[^3]

- **Crohn's disease** can affect any part of the tract from mouth to anus, most often the end of the small intestine and the start of the colon. Inflamed patches sit between healthy stretches and can go through the full thickness of the bowel wall.[^1]
- **Ulcerative colitis** affects only the large intestine. Inflammation starts at the rectum, spreads upward in a continuous stretch and involves only the inner lining.[^2]

IBD is not the same as [irritable bowel syndrome](/conditions/irritable-bowel-syndrome), which causes similar discomfort without inflammation or damage.[^3] It most often starts between the mid-teens and mid-30s, although it can begin at any age.[^1][^2]

## Symptoms

The main symptoms are diarrhea that lasts for weeks, belly pain, blood or mucus in the stool, tiredness and weight loss.[^3] Ulcerative colitis often brings an urgent, frequent need to go.[^2] Crohn's disease can also cause problems around the anus, including abscesses and fistulas, which are abnormal tunnels between the bowel and the skin or other organs.[^1]

Symptoms come in flares, with quieter periods of remission in between. Inflammation can also show up outside the gut as joint pain, sore red eyes, mouth ulcers or skin rashes.[^1][^2]

## Causes and risk factors

The exact cause is unknown. The leading explanation is an abnormal immune reaction to gut bacteria in people whose genes make them susceptible.[^1] Having a close relative with IBD raises your risk.[^1][^2]

Smoking may double the risk of Crohn's disease and makes it harder to control.[^1][^5] Stress and particular foods do not cause IBD, although they can make symptoms worse.[^2]

## Diagnosis

No single test confirms IBD. Blood tests look for anemia and inflammation. Stool tests rule out infection and measure calprotectin, a protein that rises when the gut is inflamed.[^3][^4]

The key test is a colonoscopy, in which a flexible camera examines the bowel and takes small tissue samples. Scans such as CT or MRI show parts of the small intestine that a colonoscopy cannot reach.[^1][^4]

## Treatment

Treatment aims to bring inflammation under control, keep it there and let the bowel lining heal.[^4] The main medicines are:[^4][^5]

- **Aminosalicylates** (such as mesalamine), mainly for mild to moderate ulcerative colitis.
- **Corticosteroids** to settle a flare. They are not suitable for long-term control.
- **Immunomodulators**, such as azathioprine and methotrexate.
- **Biologics and newer targeted tablets**, which block specific parts of the immune response, for moderate to severe disease.

Some people need surgery. In ulcerative colitis, removing the colon and rectum removes the disease, at the cost of a stoma or an internal pouch. In Crohn's disease, surgery removes damaged sections or treats fistulas and narrowings, but the disease can return.[^1][^2]

## Prevention and self-care

IBD cannot be prevented, but some steps reduce flares and complications. If you have Crohn's disease and smoke, stopping lowers the chance of flares and of the disease returning after surgery.[^5] Keep taking maintenance medicine when you feel well, and ask before using anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen, which can aggravate the disease.[^5]

No single diet treats IBD, though a dietitian can help you eat well through flares.[^1] Long-standing colitis raises the risk of [colorectal cancer](/conditions/colorectal-cancer), so guidelines advise regular surveillance colonoscopies starting about eight years after diagnosis.[^4]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you have blood in your stool, diarrhea lasting more than a week, belly pain that keeps coming back or unexplained weight loss.[^3]

Call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department for severe belly pain with a swollen abdomen, heavy rectal bleeding, frequent bloody diarrhea with a fever, or persistent vomiting with no stool or gas passing. These can signal a severe flare, a blockage or a perforation.[^2][^1]
