## What it is

Menopause is the point at which menstrual periods stop for good, because the ovaries have stopped releasing eggs and estrogen levels have fallen. It is confirmed in hindsight, after 12 months in a row without a period. Most women reach it between the ages of 45 and 55.[^1]

The years leading up to it are called perimenopause, or the menopausal transition. Hormone levels swing unpredictably, periods change and symptoms often begin. This phase usually lasts about seven years but can last as long as 14.[^2]

Menopause before 45 is called early, and before 40 it is called premature ovarian insufficiency.[^4]

Menopause is a normal stage of life, not an illness. The symptoms can still be hard to live with, and effective treatments exist.

## Symptoms

Symptoms vary widely:[^1][^6]

- periods that become irregular, heavier or lighter before they stop
- hot flashes and night sweats
- trouble sleeping
- mood changes, anxiety and low mood
- problems with memory and concentration, often called brain fog
- vaginal dryness, discomfort during sex and urinary symptoms
- lower sex drive, joint aches and palpitations

Hot flashes fade with time, but vaginal and urinary symptoms tend to persist or get worse without treatment.[^3] After menopause, lower estrogen also speeds up bone loss, which raises the risk of [osteoporosis](/conditions/osteoporosis).[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

Natural menopause happens when the ovaries run out of functioning egg follicles with age.[^1] It comes abruptly if both ovaries are removed, and can be brought on by chemotherapy or radiotherapy. Premature ovarian insufficiency can be linked to genetic or autoimmune conditions, though often no cause is found.[^1]

## Diagnosis

If you are over 45 and have typical symptoms, menopause or perimenopause is diagnosed from your symptoms and changes in your periods. UK guidance says blood tests are not needed.[^4] A blood test for follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) may be used for women aged 40 to 45 with symptoms, and when menopause is suspected before 40.[^4]

Pregnancy is still possible during perimenopause, so contraception is recommended until 12 months after your last period.[^1]

## Treatment

Not everyone needs treatment. For those who do, the main options are:[^3][^4][^5]

- **Hormone therapy (HRT):** estrogen, with a progestogen added for anyone who still has a uterus. It is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats and it prevents bone loss. The Menopause Society concludes that for healthy women with troublesome symptoms who are under 60, or within 10 years of menopause, the benefits generally outweigh the risks. Those risks depend on the type, dose and duration and include small increases in breast cancer, blood clots and stroke, so the decision is an individual one.
- **Vaginal estrogen:** low-dose creams, tablets or rings for dryness and urinary symptoms, with very little reaching the bloodstream.
- **Non-hormonal medicines:** certain antidepressants, gabapentin, oxybutynin and fezolinetant, a newer medicine that acts on the brain's temperature control.
- **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT),** which can make hot flashes, poor sleep and low mood less disruptive.

The Menopause Society does not recommend herbal supplements for hot flashes, or custom-compounded "bioidentical" hormones, because good evidence of benefit and safety is lacking.[^3][^5]

## Prevention and self-care

Menopause cannot be prevented, but everyday habits help with symptoms and protect long-term health:[^6]

- regular exercise, including weight-bearing and strength training for your bones
- food rich in calcium, and not smoking
- light layers, a cool bedroom and a fan for hot flashes
- vaginal moisturizers and lubricants for dryness

[Perimenopause and Menopause: Symptoms and Your Options](/articles/perimenopause-and-menopause) goes through the choices in more detail.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if symptoms are affecting your sleep, work, relationships or mood, or if your periods stop or symptoms start before age 45.[^6]

Always get checked for any vaginal bleeding after menopause. During perimenopause, see a doctor about very heavy bleeding, bleeding that lasts longer than usual, periods less than three weeks apart, and bleeding between periods or after sex.[^7]
