## What it is

Fungal skin infections are caused by fungi that live on the outer layer of the skin, hair and nails. Doctors call them tinea and name them by location: ringworm on the body, athlete's foot on the feet and jock itch in the groin, along with scalp and nail infections.[^1][^5] Despite the name, no worm is involved.[^4]

They are very common, spread easily and are usually more irritating than serious.

## Symptoms

What you see depends on where the infection is:[^1][^2][^3]

- **Body (ringworm):** a round, usually itchy patch with a raised, scaly edge and a clearer center. It looks red on lighter skin and brown or gray on darker skin.
- **Feet (athlete's foot):** itchy, white, flaky or cracked skin between the toes, which can spread to the soles and sides of the feet and sometimes forms blisters.
- **Groin (jock itch):** an itchy, scaly rash in the skin folds of the inner thighs.
- **Scalp:** scaly, itchy patches with hair loss, mainly in children.
- **Nails:** thickening, discoloration and crumbling.

## Causes and risk factors

These fungi thrive where it is warm and damp. You can pick them up from:[^1][^2]

- skin contact with a person who has the infection
- animals, especially cats and dogs
- shared towels, bedding, combs and clothing
- damp floors in showers, pools and changing rooms

Risk goes up with heavy sweating, tight shoes or clothing, walking barefoot in communal showers, contact sports such as wrestling, and a weakened immune system.[^1]

## Diagnosis

A doctor or pharmacist can often recognize a fungal rash by sight. To confirm it, a small scraping of skin or a nail clipping can be examined under a microscope or sent to a laboratory.[^1] Confirmation matters when a rash is not responding, because [eczema](/conditions/eczema) and [psoriasis](/conditions/psoriasis) can look similar and need different treatment.

## Treatment

- **Body, feet and groin:** antifungal creams, sprays or powders from a pharmacy, such as clotrimazole, miconazole or terbinafine, used as directed on the label. Treatment typically lasts two to four weeks, and it is important to finish the course even when the rash looks better, or it tends to return.[^1][^2]
- **Scalp and nails:** creams do not reach these infections, so doctors prescribe antifungal tablets, often for weeks to months.[^1]

Avoid steroid creams such as hydrocortisone unless a doctor advises them. Used on a fungal rash, they can make it worse and harder to recognize.[^1] The CDC has also reported ringworm strains that do not respond to standard antifungal treatment, so a widespread rash that is not improving needs medical review.[^1]

## Prevention and self-care

Keep skin clean and dry, especially between the toes and in skin folds:[^1][^2][^3]

- Dry your feet thoroughly after washing, and put on clean socks every day.
- Wear breathable shoes and alternate pairs so they can dry out.
- Wear flip-flops in communal showers, pools and changing rooms.
- Do not share towels, clothing, combs or sports gear.
- Wash towels and bedding regularly while you have an infection.
- Wash your hands after touching the rash or handling pets, and take a pet with patchy hair loss to a vet.

Treat athlete's foot promptly and use a separate towel for your feet, so the fungus does not spread to your groin or nails.[^3]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if the rash has not improved with a pharmacy treatment used as directed, if it is spreading or widespread, if it is on your scalp or nails, or if you have a weakened immune system.[^1][^2] If you have diabetes, get any foot problem checked, because foot infections can become serious.[^3]

Get urgent care if the skin around the rash becomes hot, swollen and painful, if redness spreads quickly, or if you develop a fever. Cracked skin can let bacteria in and cause a deeper infection.[^3]
