## What it is

Metabolic syndrome is not one disease. It is a cluster of five risk factors that tend to travel together: a large waist, raised triglycerides (a blood fat), low HDL cholesterol, high blood pressure and raised fasting blood sugar. Having any three counts as metabolic syndrome.[^1][^2]

The label is useful because the combination is riskier than its parts. People with metabolic syndrome have about twice the risk of cardiovascular disease over the next 5 to 10 years and about five times the risk of type 2 diabetes.[^2] About 1 in 3 adults in the US meets the definition.[^1]

## Symptoms

Most of the components cause no symptoms, so the syndrome is usually found by measuring. A large waistline is the one visible sign.[^4] If blood sugar is very high, it can cause thirst, frequent urination, tiredness and blurred vision.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

The two main drivers are excess fat around the abdomen and insulin resistance, where cells respond poorly to the hormone that moves glucose out of the blood.[^2]

Risk rises with age, physical inactivity, poor sleep, smoking and a family history of type 2 diabetes. It is higher in some ethnic groups, in women after menopause, and in people with polycystic ovary syndrome or sleep apnea. Some medicines, including certain treatments for depression, schizophrenia and HIV, can contribute.[^1]

## Diagnosis

A 2009 joint statement from international heart and diabetes organizations defines metabolic syndrome as any three of these five:[^2]

| Measure | Counts if |
| --- | --- |
| Waist | 102 cm (40 in) or more in men, 88 cm (35 in) or more in women |
| Triglycerides | 150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L) or higher |
| HDL cholesterol | Below 40 mg/dL (1.0 mmol/L) in men, below 50 mg/dL (1.3 mmol/L) in women |
| Blood pressure | 130 mm Hg or higher systolic, or 85 mm Hg or higher diastolic |
| Fasting glucose | 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) or higher |

Taking medicine for a component counts as having it. The waist figures shown are the US cut-offs. The statement uses lower ones for some populations, such as 90 cm (about 35 in) for men and 80 cm (about 31.5 in) for women of Asian descent.[^2]

You can check two of the five at home with our [blood pressure checker](/tools/blood-pressure) and [waist-to-height calculator](/tools/waist-to-height).

## Treatment

There is no single medicine for metabolic syndrome. Treatment tackles the shared causes first, through regular physical activity, a heart-healthy eating pattern, weight loss if you have overweight, better sleep and stopping smoking.[^1] Even modest weight loss improves triglycerides and blood sugar, and larger losses help blood pressure and cholesterol too.[^1]

If those changes are not enough, doctors treat each component on its merits, with medicines to lower blood pressure, cholesterol or blood sugar as needed.[^1][^3]

## Prevention and self-care

The same habits prevent it. Aim for regular activity ([How Much Exercise Do You Need?](/articles/how-much-exercise-do-you-need) covers the targets), an eating pattern rich in vegetables, whole grains, beans, fish and unsaturated fats such as the [Mediterranean diet](/articles/mediterranean-diet-guide), and enough sleep.[^1]

It also helps to know your own figures. [Know Your Numbers](/articles/know-your-numbers) explains the handful of measurements worth tracking.

## When to see a doctor

Ask for a check of your blood pressure, cholesterol, triglycerides and blood sugar if your waist is above the thresholds, if type 2 diabetes or early heart disease runs in your family, or if you already know one of the five is raised.[^3]

Metabolic syndrome itself is not an emergency, but its consequences can be. Chest pain or pressure, sudden weakness on one side, face drooping or trouble speaking need your local emergency number straight away.
