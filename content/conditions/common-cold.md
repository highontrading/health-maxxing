## What it is

The common cold is a mild viral infection of the nose and throat. More than 200 different viruses can cause it, most often rhinoviruses.[^1] That variety is why you can catch cold after cold, and why there is no vaccine.[^1]

Adults average two to three colds a year, and children have more. Most people recover in about 7 to 10 days, although a cough can linger.[^1]

## Symptoms

Symptoms come on gradually over two or three days: a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, a sore throat, a cough, hoarseness and feeling tired.[^2] Adults seldom run much of a fever with a cold; children are more likely to.[^2] Mucus often starts out clear and turns thicker and yellow or green after a few days. That is normal and does not mean you need antibiotics.[^1]

A sudden high fever, marked body aches and exhaustion point more toward flu or COVID-19. [Cold, Flu or COVID?](/articles/cold-flu-or-covid) explains how to tell them apart.

## Causes and risk factors

Cold viruses spread through the air when a person with a cold coughs or sneezes, through close contact, and on hands that touch a contaminated surface and then the eyes, nose or mouth.[^1] Colds can happen at any time of year but are most common in the colder months. Young children, and the adults who spend time around them, catch the most.[^1][^3]

## Diagnosis

A cold is diagnosed from its symptoms, and tests are not usually needed.[^3] If your illness looks like something else, a swab can check for flu, COVID-19 or [strep throat](/conditions/strep-throat).

## Treatment

There is no cure for a cold, and antibiotics do not work against viruses. Taking them brings a risk of side effects without any benefit.[^1] Treatment is about feeling better while your immune system clears the infection:[^1][^2]

- rest and plenty of fluids
- pain and fever relievers such as acetaminophen (paracetamol) or ibuprofen, used as directed on the label
- saline nose drops or spray, and a clean humidifier
- honey for a cough, for adults and children over 1 year; never give honey to a baby under 12 months

Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines are not recommended for young children, and age limits vary by country, so check the label and ask a pharmacist. Never give aspirin to children or teenagers.[^1]

Vitamin C is the most studied cold remedy. A Cochrane review of trials found that taking it regularly did not prevent colds in the general population. It shortened them slightly, by about 8% in adults and 14% in children, and starting it only after symptoms began showed no consistent benefit.[^4] For more on what does and does not help, see [Can You Really Boost Your Immune System?](/articles/can-you-boost-your-immune-system).

## Prevention and self-care

Wash your hands often with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, avoid touching your eyes, nose and mouth with unwashed hands, and keep some distance from people who are sick.[^1] When you have a cold, cover coughs and sneezes, clean surfaces that are touched a lot, and stay home while you feel unwell if you can.[^1]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if:[^1]

- symptoms last more than 10 days without improving
- a fever lasts longer than four days
- symptoms improve and then return or get worse
- you are becoming dehydrated
- a long-term condition, such as asthma or heart disease, is getting worse

Get urgent care for difficulty breathing or fast breathing. A baby younger than 3 months with a fever of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher needs to be seen straight away.[^1] [Fever: What It Means and When to Worry](/articles/fever-when-to-worry) covers other warning signs.
