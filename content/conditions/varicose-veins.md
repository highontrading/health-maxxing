## What it is

Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins that bulge just under the skin, usually on the legs. Leg veins contain one-way valves that keep blood moving up toward the heart. When the valves weaken, blood flows backward and pools, and the vein stretches.[^1][^2]

They are common and, for most people, more of a nuisance than a health threat. They rarely cause serious problems and often need no treatment.[^1]

## Symptoms

Besides the look of the veins, you may notice:[^1]

- Aching, heavy or uncomfortable legs.
- Swollen feet and ankles.
- Burning or throbbing in the legs.
- Muscle cramps, particularly at night.
- Dry, itchy, thin skin over the affected vein.

Symptoms are usually worse in warm weather or after standing for a long time, and improve when you walk around or rest with your legs raised.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

Anything that raises pressure in the leg veins or weakens their walls increases the risk: older age, being female, a family history, pregnancy, excess weight and jobs that involve long periods of standing.[^1][^4] A previous clot in a deep vein can also damage the valves.[^2]

## Diagnosis

A doctor can usually diagnose varicose veins by looking at your legs while you stand. If treatment is being considered, a duplex ultrasound scan shows the direction of blood flow and which valves are leaking.[^1][^3]

## Treatment

Veins that cause no discomfort do not need treating.[^1] For those that do, the UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) recommends, in this order:[^3]

1. **Endothermal ablation**, which seals the vein from the inside using heat from radiofrequency or laser energy.
2. **Foam sclerotherapy**, an injection of foam that scars the vein closed, if ablation is not suitable.
3. **Surgery** to tie off and remove the vein, if neither of the others is suitable.

NICE advises against relying on compression stockings as the treatment unless these procedures are unsuitable. Stockings can still ease swelling and aching, including during pregnancy, when procedures are usually postponed.[^3]

## Prevention and self-care

There is little evidence that you can prevent varicose veins or stop them from getting worse. These steps can ease symptoms:[^1]

- Avoid standing or sitting still for long periods, and move around regularly. Our guide to [breaking up long spells of sitting](/articles/sitting-all-day-what-to-do) has practical ideas.
- Raise your legs on pillows when you rest.
- Exercise regularly, which improves circulation and helps you keep a healthy weight.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if your veins are painful or uncomfortable, the skin over them is sore or irritated, or the aching disturbs your sleep.[^1] Get prompt advice if you develop a sore near the ankle that does not heal, skin that changes color or hardens, or a vein that becomes hard, hot and painful.[^3]

If a varicose vein bleeds, lie down, raise the leg and press firmly on the spot. **Call your local emergency number if the bleeding does not stop.**[^1]

A leg that suddenly becomes swollen, painful and warm may mean a [deep vein thrombosis](/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis), which needs assessment the same day. With breathlessness or chest pain, treat it as an emergency.
