## What it is

Melanoma is a cancer that starts in melanocytes, the cells that make the pigment melanin. It makes up only about 1% of skin cancers but causes most skin cancer deaths, because it is more likely to spread.[^1]

About 7 in 10 melanomas appear as a new spot on normal-looking skin; the rest arise within an existing mole.[^8] They can develop anywhere, including places that get little sun. In people with darker skin, melanoma most often shows up on the palms, the soles of the feet or under the nails.[^7]

## Symptoms

The key sign is a spot that is new or changing. The ABCDE rule describes what to look for:[^3]

| Letter | Warning sign |
| --- | --- |
| A, asymmetry | One half does not match the other |
| B, border | Irregular, notched or blurred edges |
| C, color | Several shades in one spot |
| D, diameter | Usually over 6 mm (1/4 inch), but can be smaller |
| E, evolving | Changing in size, shape or color |

Also watch for an "ugly duckling" (a mole that looks unlike your others), a sore that does not heal, a mole that itches or bleeds, and a dark streak under a nail that was not caused by an injury.[^1][^4] Some melanomas are pink or skin-colored, so judge by change, not just darkness.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun and from tanning beds is the main avoidable cause.[^5] Your risk is higher if you have:[^1][^2]

- fair skin that burns easily, or light-colored hair or eyes
- a history of sunburns, especially blistering burns when young
- many moles, or moles that look unusual
- a close relative with melanoma, or a previous melanoma yourself
- a weakened immune system

Darker skin lowers the risk but does not remove it, and melanoma in darker skin is often found at a later stage.[^7]

## Diagnosis

A doctor examines the spot, often with a handheld magnifier called a dermatoscope. If it looks suspicious, it is removed and examined under a microscope. This biopsy is the only way to confirm melanoma.[^2][^4]

How deeply the tumor has grown into the skin matters most: thinner melanomas have a much better outlook.[^1] Depending on the depth, nearby lymph nodes may be checked and scans used to look for spread.[^2]

## Treatment

Surgery to remove the melanoma with a margin of normal skin is the main treatment, and often the only one needed for early melanoma.[^2] When melanoma has spread, or is at high risk of returning, options include:[^1][^2]

- **Immunotherapy,** medicines that help the immune system attack the cancer.
- **Targeted therapy** for tumors with certain gene changes, such as BRAF mutations.
- **Radiotherapy or chemotherapy** in some situations.

In US data, the five-year relative survival rate is above 99% for melanoma found before it has spread, and much lower once it has reached distant organs.[^1]

## Prevention and self-care

- Seek shade when the sun is strong, cover up with clothing, a hat and sunglasses, and use sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher on exposed skin.[^4] Our [sunscreen guide](/articles/sunscreen-guide) explains how to use it properly.
- Do not use tanning beds.[^5]
- Get to know your skin, including your back, scalp, soles and nails. [Skin Cancer and Mole Checks](/articles/skin-cancer-and-mole-checks) shows how to check it.

The US Preventive Services Task Force concluded in 2023 that there is not enough evidence to recommend for or against routine skin checks by a clinician in adults without symptoms.[^6] If you are at higher risk, ask your doctor what schedule suits you.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor promptly about a mole that has changed size, shape or color, a mole that is itchy, painful, bleeding or crusty, a new or unusual mark that has not gone away after a few weeks, or a dark area under a nail with no injury to explain it.[^4]

Melanoma found early is much easier to treat, so do not wait to see what happens.[^4]
