## What it is

The carpal tunnel is a narrow passage on the palm side of the wrist, formed by the wrist bones and a tough ligament that stretches across them. The median nerve and the tendons that bend the fingers run through it. Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) happens when the nerve is squeezed in this tight space.[^1][^3]

The median nerve supplies feeling to the thumb, index finger, middle finger and part of the ring finger, and it powers muscles at the base of the thumb. That is why the little finger is spared.[^3]

## Symptoms

Symptoms usually come on gradually:[^1][^2][^3]

- numbness, tingling or burning in the thumb and the next three fingers
- symptoms that are worse at night and may wake you, with relief from shaking the hand
- tingling when you hold a phone, a book or a steering wheel
- an ache that can travel up the forearm
- later on, a weak grip, clumsiness and dropping things

In long-standing cases the muscles at the base of the thumb can waste away.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

Anything that narrows the tunnel or swells its contents can press on the nerve:[^1][^2][^3]

- **Sex and family history.** Women are about three times more likely than men to develop CTS, and it can run in families.
- **Pregnancy.** Symptoms often improve after the birth.
- **Health conditions,** including diabetes, an underactive thyroid, [rheumatoid arthritis](/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis) and obesity.
- **A wrist injury,** such as a fracture.
- **Work or hobbies** that involve gripping hard, bending the wrist repeatedly or using vibrating tools.

CTS appears to be more common in assembly-line work than in keyboard work.[^1]

## Diagnosis

A clinician will test feeling and strength in your hand and may try to bring on the tingling by tapping over the nerve at the wrist or holding your wrist in a bent position.[^3] Nerve conduction studies, which measure how fast signals travel along the nerve, can confirm the diagnosis and show how severe it is.[^1][^3]

## Treatment

Mild or recent symptoms are usually treated without surgery first:[^2][^3]

- **A wrist splint** that keeps the wrist straight, worn mainly at night. The NHS advises giving it at least four weeks to work.
- **Changing or pausing activities** that bring symptoms on.
- **A steroid injection** into the carpal tunnel, which can relieve symptoms, though they may return.

Painkillers such as ibuprofen may ease discomfort for a short time, but there is little evidence that they treat the cause.[^2]

If symptoms are severe, keep coming back, or include signs of nerve damage such as constant numbness or muscle wasting, a surgeon can perform a carpal tunnel release. This divides the ligament that forms the roof of the tunnel, giving the nerve more room. It is usually done under local anesthetic without an overnight stay, by an open or keyhole (endoscopic) technique.[^2][^3]

Surgery improves symptoms for most people. Grip strength can take a few months to return, and when the nerve has been badly compressed for a long time, recovery may be slow or incomplete.[^3]

## Prevention and self-care

There is no guaranteed way to prevent CTS, but you can reduce strain on the wrist: take frequent breaks from repetitive tasks, keep your wrist in a relaxed, straight position, and ease your grip where you can.[^1] For desk setup, see [Posture and 'Tech Neck'](/articles/posture-and-tech-neck).

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if numbness, tingling or weakness in your hand keeps coming back, wakes you at night or affects daily tasks. Do not wait if the numbness has become constant or your thumb is getting weak. Early diagnosis and treatment are important to prevent permanent nerve damage.[^4]

Call your local emergency number if numbness or weakness in a hand or arm comes on suddenly, especially with face drooping or trouble speaking, because this could be a stroke. Severe new numbness after a wrist injury needs same-day assessment.
