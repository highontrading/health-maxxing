## What it is

Lung cancer begins when cells in the lung grow out of control and form a tumor. It is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide, with about 2.5 million new cases and 1.8 million deaths in 2022.[^1][^7]

There are two main types. Non-small cell lung cancer accounts for about 80 to 85% of cases. Small cell lung cancer is less common and tends to grow and spread faster.[^4]

## Symptoms

Early lung cancer often causes no symptoms, which is one reason many cases are found late.[^1] When symptoms appear, they include:[^5]

- A cough that lasts more than three weeks, or a long-standing cough that gets worse.
- Coughing up blood.
- Persistent breathlessness.
- An ache or pain when breathing or coughing.
- Lung infections that keep coming back.
- Unexplained weight loss, loss of appetite or persistent tiredness.

These symptoms usually have another cause, but they need checking.

## Causes and risk factors

Smoking is responsible for about 85% of cases.[^1] The risk rises with how much and how long you have smoked, and falls after you quit.[^3]

People who have never smoked get lung cancer too. Other causes include secondhand smoke, radon (a natural radioactive gas that can build up indoors), asbestos and other workplace hazards, air pollution and a family history.[^1][^3]

## Diagnosis

A chest X-ray or CT scan usually comes first. If it shows something suspicious, a biopsy (a small tissue sample) confirms the diagnosis and the type. Further scans show how far the cancer has spread, and the tumor is tested for gene changes and proteins that guide treatment.[^3][^4]

**Screening.** For people at high risk, yearly low-dose CT scans can find lung cancer early, when it is more treatable. In a large US trial, CT screening cut lung cancer deaths by about 20% compared with chest X-ray.[^6] The US Preventive Services Task Force (2021) recommends yearly screening for adults aged 50 to 80 who have a 20 pack-year smoking history and currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years.[^2] A pack-year is one pack a day for a year. Programs differ by country; [Cancer Screening, Explained](/articles/cancer-screening-explained) weighs the benefits and limits.

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of the cancer and on your general health:[^3][^4]

- **Surgery** to remove part of the lung, mainly for early non-small cell cancers.
- **Radiation therapy**, alone or combined with chemotherapy.
- **Chemotherapy.**
- **Targeted therapy**, medicines aimed at specific gene changes in the tumor.
- **Immunotherapy**, which helps your immune system attack the cancer.

Care to control symptoms can be given alongside any of these.[^3]

## Prevention and self-care

Not smoking is by far the most effective protection, and quitting at any age lowers your risk.[^1][^3] [How to Quit Smoking (and Vaping)](/articles/how-to-quit-smoking-and-vaping) ranks the methods by evidence.

You can also avoid secondhand smoke, test your home for radon where it is common, and follow safety rules if you work with asbestos, dust or fumes.[^3] If you smoke, do not take beta-carotene supplements: trials found that they raise the risk of lung cancer.[^3]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you have a cough that has lasted more than three weeks, cough up blood, or have unexplained breathlessness, chest pain or weight loss.[^5] If you smoke or used to, ask whether you qualify for screening.

**Seek emergency care** if you cough up a lot of blood or have severe difficulty breathing. In someone with lung cancer, swelling of the face and neck, or new leg weakness with loss of bladder or bowel control, is also an emergency.
