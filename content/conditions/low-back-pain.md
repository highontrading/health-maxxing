## What it is

Low back pain is pain felt between the lower edge of the ribs and the buttocks. It is called acute when it lasts less than 6 weeks, subacute from 6 to 12 weeks, and chronic beyond 12 weeks.[^1]

It is the leading cause of disability worldwide and affected 619 million people in 2020.[^1] In about 90% of cases it is "non-specific", which means no particular disease or structural problem can be identified as the cause.[^1] That does not mean the pain is imagined. It means nothing dangerous has been found.

## Symptoms

The pain can be a dull ache or sharp, often with stiffness or muscle spasm, and it may spread to the buttocks or thighs. It usually changes with movement and position.[^1][^5] Pain, numbness or tingling that travels below the knee suggests an irritated nerve (see [sciatica](/conditions/sciatica)).

## Causes and risk factors

Most back pain involves the muscles, ligaments, discs and joints of the spine, without a single identifiable culprit.[^1] Specific causes are less common. They include a slipped disc pressing on a nerve, narrowing of the spinal canal, a fracture in a spinal bone, inflammatory arthritis of the spine and, rarely, infection or cancer.[^5]

Risk is higher with low physical activity, smoking, obesity and physically demanding work.[^1]

## Diagnosis

A clinician diagnoses back pain from your history and an examination, checking for warning signs and for nerve problems. Scans are not routinely needed. NICE advises against routine imaging outside specialist care, and recommends it only when the result is likely to change how you are treated.[^2]

## Treatment

Most new episodes improve within a few weeks.[^4] The priorities are:

- **Stay active.** Keep moving and return to normal activities gradually. Long spells in bed slow recovery.[^4]
- **Exercise.** NICE recommends a group exercise program (strengthening, aerobic, mind-body or a mix), chosen to suit your needs and preferences.[^2]
- **Other non-drug options.** The American College of Physicians recommends trying these first: heat, massage, acupuncture or spinal manipulation for recent pain, and exercise, rehabilitation programs, mindfulness-based stress reduction, tai chi or yoga for chronic pain.[^3]
- **Psychological approaches** such as cognitive behavioral therapy, combined with exercise, when pain persists.[^2]

If medicine is needed, guidelines favor a short course of an anti-inflammatory painkiller (NSAID) at the lowest effective dose.[^2][^3] NICE advises against paracetamol (acetaminophen) on its own, and against opioids for chronic low back pain.[^2]

Guidelines differ on acupuncture: the American College of Physicians includes it, while NICE does not recommend it. NICE also advises against belts, corsets, traction and ultrasound.[^2][^3] Surgery and spinal injections are reserved for specific problems.[^2]

## Prevention and self-care

Back pain often comes back. Staying physically active, keeping a healthy weight, not smoking, sleeping well and adjusting your work setup all help.[^1] For more, see [Low Back Pain: What Works (and What Doesn't)](/articles/low-back-pain-what-works) and [what to do about sitting all day](/articles/sitting-all-day-what-to-do).

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if the pain has not improved after a few weeks, is severe or getting worse, or stops you doing everyday activities.[^4]

Call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department if back pain comes with any of the following. They can signal cauda equina syndrome, in which the nerves at the base of the spine are compressed and urgent surgery is needed to prevent permanent damage:[^4][^6]

- numbness or tingling around your genitals or buttocks
- difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control
- pain, tingling, weakness or numbness in both legs

Get emergency help, too, for back pain with chest pain, or back pain that started after a serious accident. Back pain with a high temperature, unexplained weight loss or pain that is worse at night needs an urgent appointment.[^4]
