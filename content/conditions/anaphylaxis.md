## What it is

Anaphylaxis is a severe, whole-body allergic reaction that develops quickly, usually within minutes of contact with a trigger, and can be fatal.[^1][^2] The immune system releases a flood of chemicals that can narrow the airways, swell the throat and make blood pressure fall suddenly.[^2]

It is always a medical emergency. The first and most important treatment is an injection of epinephrine, also called adrenaline, given as early as possible.[^2]

## Symptoms

Symptoms come on fast and can affect several parts of the body at once:[^1][^2]

- **Airway and breathing:** swelling of the throat or tongue, a tight throat, a hoarse voice, trouble swallowing, wheezing, coughing or noisy breathing.
- **Circulation:** feeling faint, dizzy or confused, collapse, and cold, clammy skin. Skin, lips or tongue may look blue, gray or pale. On brown and Black skin this may be easier to see on the palms and soles.[^6]
- **Skin:** hives, itching, flushing, or swelling of the lips, face or eyes.
- **Gut:** stomach cramps, vomiting or diarrhea.

Hives are common but not always present, so their absence does not rule anaphylaxis out.[^2]

## Causes and risk factors

The most common triggers are:[^1][^3]

- foods, especially peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, fish, shellfish and sesame
- insect stings, such as those from bees and wasps
- medicines, including some antibiotics and anti-inflammatory painkillers such as aspirin and ibuprofen
- latex

Occasionally exercise is the trigger, and sometimes no cause is found.[^1][^3] A previous anaphylactic reaction and asthma, particularly when it is poorly controlled, raise the risk of a severe reaction.[^2][^4]

## Diagnosis

Anaphylaxis is diagnosed on the spot from the sudden pattern of symptoms, and treatment does not wait for tests.[^2] Blood samples taken soon afterward can measure tryptase, a chemical released during a reaction, to help confirm it.[^5] After any episode you should be referred to an allergy specialist, who uses your history with skin or blood tests to pin down the trigger.[^5]

## Treatment

If you suspect anaphylaxis, act immediately:[^1]

1. Use an epinephrine (adrenaline) auto-injector if one is available, following the instructions on the device.
2. Call your local emergency number and say you think it is anaphylaxis.
3. Lie down. You can raise your legs. If breathing is difficult, raise your shoulders or sit up slowly. If you are pregnant, lie on your left side.
4. After an insect sting, try to remove the stinger if it is still in the skin.
5. If symptoms have not improved after five minutes, use a second auto-injector.
6. Do not stand or walk at any time, even if you feel better.

Go to hospital even if the epinephrine works, because symptoms can return hours later.[^1][^5] Hospital care can include more epinephrine, oxygen and fluids into a vein, followed by a period of observation.[^1] Antihistamines and steroids are not a substitute for epinephrine and should never delay it.[^2][^3][^4]

## Prevention and self-care

Once your trigger is known, avoiding it is the foundation. Read food labels, tell restaurant staff about your allergy, and make sure every health professional knows about any medicine allergy.[^4] If you have been prescribed auto-injectors:[^1][^4]

- carry two with you at all times, and check their expiry dates
- make sure family, friends, colleagues and teachers know when and how to use them
- keep a written emergency action plan, and consider wearing medical ID jewelry

For insect sting allergy, a course of immunotherapy from a specialist can greatly reduce the risk of future reactions.[^3] Keeping asthma well controlled matters too.[^2]

## When to see a doctor

Do not wait to see whether symptoms pass. Use epinephrine and call your local emergency number at the first signs of anaphylaxis, even if you are not sure.[^1]

See a doctor soon after any reaction that involved widespread [hives](/conditions/hives), swelling, or breathing or stomach symptoms following a food, sting or medicine. You may need a referral to an allergy clinic and a prescription for auto-injectors.[^5]
