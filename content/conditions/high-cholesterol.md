## What it is

Cholesterol is a waxy, fat-like substance your body uses to build cells and make hormones. Your body makes all it needs; the rest comes from food.[^1] It travels in the blood inside particles called lipoproteins. LDL ("bad") cholesterol can lodge in artery walls and build fatty plaque, while HDL ("good") cholesterol carries it back to the liver.[^1]

High cholesterol usually means too much LDL. Over years, plaque narrows and stiffens arteries, which is what leads to [coronary artery disease](/conditions/coronary-artery-disease), [heart attack](/conditions/heart-attack) and [stroke](/conditions/stroke).[^1][^2]

## Symptoms

There are none. You can feel perfectly well with very high cholesterol, and the first sign may be a heart attack or stroke. A blood test is the only way to know.[^2][^3]

The exception is inherited high cholesterol, which can cause yellowish fatty lumps on tendons or eyelids, or a pale ring around the colored part of the eye.[^4]

## Causes and risk factors

Habits and genes both matter. LDL tends to rise with a diet high in saturated and trans fats, excess weight, physical inactivity and smoking, and it creeps up with age.[^1][^3] Type 2 diabetes, an underactive thyroid, kidney disease and some medicines can raise it too.[^1]

About 1 in 250 people have familial hypercholesterolemia, a genetic condition that causes very high LDL from birth and, untreated, heart disease at a young age.[^4]

## Diagnosis

A lipid panel measures total cholesterol, LDL, HDL and triglycerides, another blood fat. US health agencies describe these levels as healthy for adults:[^1][^2]

| Measure | Healthy level |
| --- | --- |
| Total cholesterol | Below 200 mg/dL (5.2 mmol/L) |
| LDL | Below 100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L) |
| HDL | At least 40 mg/dL (1.0 mmol/L) for men, 50 mg/dL (1.3 mmol/L) for women |
| Triglycerides | Below 150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L) |

The numbers are only part of the picture. Doctors combine them with your age, blood pressure, smoking and diabetes status to estimate your chance of a heart attack or stroke over the next 10 years, and base treatment on that overall risk.[^6] An LDL of 190 mg/dL (4.9 mmol/L) or higher is treated as severe in its own right and raises the question of an inherited cause.[^6] [Cholesterol: What LDL, HDL and Triglycerides Really Mean](/articles/cholesterol-explained) goes deeper.

## Treatment

Treatment starts with eating pattern, activity and weight, and adds medication when overall risk is high enough.[^6] Statins are the first-choice medicine: they lower LDL and reduce heart attacks and strokes. The US Preventive Services Task Force (2022) recommends a statin for adults aged 40 to 75 who have at least one cardiovascular risk factor and an estimated 10-year risk of 10% or more.[^5]

If a statin is not enough or not tolerated, doctors may add or switch to other medicines such as ezetimibe, bempedoic acid or injected PCSK9 inhibitors.[^3] People with familial hypercholesterolemia usually need medication, often from a young age.[^6]

## Prevention and self-care

- Swap saturated fats (fatty meat, butter, pastries) for unsaturated ones (olive oil, nuts, fatty fish). [Fats, Decoded](/articles/dietary-fats-decoded) explains the difference.
- Eat more fiber-rich foods such as oats, beans, fruit and vegetables.
- Be active most days, keep a healthy weight, do not smoke and go easy on alcohol.[^1][^3]

These changes help, but they cannot fully offset an inherited tendency. Needing medication is not a personal failure.

## When to see a doctor

Ask for a cholesterol test if you have never had one. In the US, most healthy adults are advised to test every 4 to 6 years, and more often with diabetes, heart disease or a family history of high cholesterol.[^2] Ask sooner if a close relative had a heart attack or stroke before about 55 (men) or 65 (women).[^6]

High cholesterol itself is never an emergency, but its consequences are. **Call your local emergency number** for chest pain or pressure that lasts more than a few minutes, or for sudden face drooping, arm weakness or trouble speaking.
