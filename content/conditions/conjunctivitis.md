## What it is

The conjunctiva is a thin, clear membrane that lines the inside of your eyelids and covers the white of your eye. When it becomes inflamed, its small blood vessels swell and the eye looks pink or red.[^2]

There are four main causes: viruses, bacteria, allergies and irritants. Viral and bacterial conjunctivitis spread easily from person to person. Allergic and irritant conjunctivitis are not contagious.[^1][^4]

## Symptoms

All types cause redness, a gritty or burning feeling, watering and some swelling of the eyelids.[^1] A few features hint at the cause, although they overlap:[^1]

- **Viral:** watery discharge, often starting in one eye and spreading to the other within days, sometimes alongside a cold or sore throat.
- **Bacterial:** thicker yellow or green discharge that can glue the eyelids together overnight.
- **Allergic:** intense itching and tearing in both eyes, often with sneezing or an itchy nose.

## Causes and risk factors

Viral conjunctivitis is often caused by adenoviruses, the same family behind many colds. Bacterial conjunctivitis is more common in children than in adults. Allergic conjunctivitis is set off by pollen, dust mites, molds or animal dander, and irritant conjunctivitis by smoke, pool chlorine or a foreign body in the eye.[^1]

The infectious types spread through hands, shared towels, eye makeup and contaminated surfaces. Wearing contact lenses, especially for long stretches, raises the risk of eye infection.[^1][^2]

Newborn babies can pick up serious eye infections during birth. These need immediate medical treatment.[^1]

## Diagnosis

A doctor can usually tell from your symptoms and an eye examination. Swabs and laboratory tests are needed only for severe, unusual or long-lasting cases.[^1]

## Treatment

Most cases need no medicine.

- **Viral.** Most cases are mild and clear in 7 to 14 days without treatment, although some take two to three weeks or longer. Antibiotics do not work against viruses. Antiviral medicine is used for more serious forms, such as those caused by herpes viruses.[^1]
- **Bacterial.** Mild cases often improve in two to five days without treatment but can take up to two weeks to go completely. Antibiotic drops or ointment may shorten the illness and reduce spread, and are more likely to be prescribed when there is pus, a weakened immune system or a particular bacterium is suspected.[^1]
- **Allergic.** It usually improves once the trigger is removed. Allergy eye drops, including antihistamines, can help.[^1]

With any type, cold compresses and lubricating eye drops (artificial tears) ease dryness and swelling.[^1]

## Prevention and self-care

Wash your hands often, avoid touching or rubbing your eyes, and wipe away discharge with a clean, damp cloth. Use your own towel and pillowcase, and do not share eye drops or makeup.[^1] Stop wearing contact lenses until your eyes are back to normal, and ask your eye care professional whether to replace the lenses and case.[^1][^4]

Rules on work and school vary. The CDC says you may be able to attend, with a doctor's approval, if you have no fever or other symptoms, but should stay home if you still have symptoms and cannot avoid close contact. The NHS says there is no need to stay away unless you or your child feels very unwell.[^1][^3]

If allergies are the cause, see [Seasonal Allergies: A Relief Plan That Works](/articles/seasonal-allergies-relief). [Antibiotics: When They Help and When They Hurt](/articles/antibiotics-when-they-help) explains why they are often not needed.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if symptoms get worse or are not improving, if you have a weakened immune system, or if a bacterial infection has not started to improve after 24 hours of antibiotics.[^1]

Get urgent, same-day care for eye pain, strong sensitivity to light, blurred vision that does not clear when you wipe the eye, or intense redness. A baby under 28 days old with red, sticky eyes also needs to be seen urgently.[^1][^3] These signs can point to conditions that threaten sight, such as a corneal infection or acute [glaucoma](/conditions/glaucoma).
