## What it is

Heart failure means your heart cannot pump enough blood to meet your body's needs, either because the muscle has become too weak to squeeze well or too stiff to fill properly. It does not mean your heart has stopped or is about to.[^1]

Doctors describe it by ejection fraction, the share of blood the main pumping chamber pushes out with each beat. In heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, that share is 40% or less. In heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, it is 50% or more and the problem is filling.[^3]

It is a long-term condition that usually cannot be cured, but treatment can control symptoms for many years and helps people live longer.[^2][^4]

## Symptoms

- Breathlessness with activity, when lying flat, or waking you at night.
- Swelling in the feet, ankles, legs or abdomen.
- Tiredness and reduced ability to exercise.
- Sudden weight gain from fluid build-up.
- A persistent cough or wheeze.
- A fast or irregular heartbeat.[^1][^2]

Symptoms may develop gradually over months or come on quickly.[^4]

## Causes and risk factors

Heart failure is usually the end result of conditions that damage or overwork the heart: [coronary artery disease](/conditions/coronary-artery-disease) and past heart attacks, [high blood pressure](/conditions/high-blood-pressure), diabetes, heart valve problems, heart muscle disease (cardiomyopathy) and rhythm problems such as [atrial fibrillation](/conditions/atrial-fibrillation).[^1][^4] Obesity, heavy drinking and some cancer treatments also raise the risk.[^1]

## Diagnosis

Alongside an examination, the key tests are:[^3][^4]

- A blood test for natriuretic peptides (BNP or NT-proBNP), hormones the heart releases under strain.
- An echocardiogram, an ultrasound scan that shows how well the heart pumps and fills and measures ejection fraction.
- An ECG and chest X-ray to look for rhythm problems, past heart attacks and fluid in the lungs.

## Treatment

For reduced ejection fraction, the 2022 US guideline recommends four types of medicine that each help people live longer: a drug that relaxes blood vessels (an ARNI, ACE inhibitor or ARB), a beta blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist and an SGLT2 inhibitor.[^3] Diuretics ("water pills") relieve fluid build-up.

For preserved ejection fraction, SGLT2 inhibitors and diuretics are the main medicines, alongside treatment of blood pressure and other contributing conditions.[^3]

Some people benefit from devices: an implanted defibrillator that corrects dangerous rhythms, or a special pacemaker that helps the heart beat in a coordinated way. Advanced heart failure may be treated with a mechanical pump or a heart transplant.[^1][^3] Supervised exercise in a cardiac rehabilitation program improves fitness and quality of life.[^3]

## Prevention and self-care

Controlling blood pressure, not smoking, staying active and managing diabetes and cholesterol lower your risk of developing heart failure.[^1] If you have it:

- Take your medicines as prescribed and keep follow-up appointments.
- Weigh yourself each morning. The American Heart Association advises reporting a gain of about 1 to 1.5 kg (2 to 3 pounds) in a day, or 2.3 kg (5 pounds) in a week, because it signals fluid build-up.[^2]
- Follow your team's advice on salt and fluids, limit alcohol and keep up to date with flu and pneumococcal vaccines.[^2][^4] [The DASH Diet and Salt](/articles/dash-diet-and-salt) shows where salt hides in food.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor promptly for new or worsening breathlessness, ankle swelling or unexplained tiredness.[^4] If you already have heart failure, call your care team about rapid weight gain, increasing swelling, or needing more pillows to breathe comfortably at night.[^2]

**Call your local emergency number** for severe breathlessness or struggling to breathe at rest, coughing up pink frothy mucus, chest pain, fainting, or a very fast or irregular heartbeat with breathlessness or dizziness.[^2]
