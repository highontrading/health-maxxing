## What it is

Testosterone is the main male sex hormone, made mostly in the testicles. It fuels sex drive and sperm production and helps maintain muscle and bone.[^6]

Male hypogonadism means the testicles do not make enough testosterone, sperm or both. The problem may lie in the testicles themselves (primary) or in the pituitary gland and hypothalamus, the parts of the brain that signal to them (secondary).[^1]

Testosterone also drifts down by about 1% a year from around the age of 30 to 40. That gradual decline is normal and is unlikely to cause problems in itself.[^5]

## Symptoms

The most telling symptoms are sexual: a reduced sex drive, fewer morning erections and erectile dysfunction.[^1] Others are less specific and often have other causes:[^1][^4]

- Tiredness, low mood and poor concentration.
- Loss of muscle and strength, and more body fat.
- Loss of body hair, breast enlargement or hot flashes.
- Infertility and low bone density.

## Causes and risk factors

- **Problems in the testicles**: genetic conditions such as Klinefelter syndrome, injury, infection, chemotherapy or radiation therapy.[^1][^4]
- **Problems with pituitary signaling**: pituitary tumors, a high prolactin level, iron overload, and medicines such as opioids, corticosteroids and anabolic steroids.[^1]
- **Other health conditions.** Obesity, type 2 diabetes and [sleep apnea](/conditions/sleep-apnea) are linked to lower testosterone, which can recover when they are treated.[^1][^2]

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis needs both symptoms and blood tests. A number alone is not enough.[^1] Testosterone is measured in the morning, when levels are highest, and a low result has to be confirmed with a second test on another day.[^1][^2]

| Guideline | Total testosterone considered low |
| --- | --- |
| American Urological Association (2018) | Below 300 ng/dL (10.4 nmol/L) |
| Endocrine Society (2018) | Below 264 ng/dL (9.2 nmol/L), the lower limit for healthy young men |

Further blood tests, and sometimes a scan of the pituitary gland, look for the cause.[^1] The Endocrine Society advises against routinely testing men who have no symptoms.[^1]

## Treatment

A reversible cause is tackled first. Losing excess weight or changing a medicine can raise testosterone.[^1][^2]

Testosterone therapy, as a gel, injection or other form, is recommended for men with confirmed hypogonadism. It improves sex drive and, more modestly, erectile function, and helps correct anemia and low bone density. Evidence that it improves energy or thinking is weak, and any effect on mood is small.[^1]

It also has limits and risks:[^1][^3]

- It suppresses sperm production, so it is not suitable for men who are trying to conceive.
- It can raise the red blood cell count, which thickens the blood, so blood counts, testosterone levels and PSA are monitored.
- Guidelines advise against it for men with prostate or breast cancer, or a recent heart attack or stroke.
- In the TRAVERSE trial of men aged 45 to 80 at high cardiovascular risk, testosterone gel did not increase heart attacks or strokes compared with placebo. Atrial fibrillation, blood clots in the lungs and acute kidney injury were more common.

The Endocrine Society does not recommend testosterone for age-related decline in men without clear symptoms and consistently low levels.[^1] [Testosterone: What's Normal, What's Low and What TRT Can Do](/articles/testosterone-explained) covers the evidence in detail.

## Prevention and self-care

You cannot prevent genetic or pituitary causes. For the rest, keeping a healthy weight and staying physically active support normal testosterone levels.[^2] Avoid anabolic steroids, which shut down your body's own production.[^1]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you have a persistently low sex drive, erection problems, breast changes or difficulty conceiving.[^4] Get tested before using any testosterone product: starting treatment without a confirmed diagnosis can mask the real cause and switch off your own production.[^1] If fertility is the concern, [Male Fertility: The Basics Nobody Told You](/articles/male-fertility-basics) explains the tests.

**Seek emergency care** for a sudden severe headache with loss of vision or double vision, which can signal a pituitary problem. If you use testosterone, do the same for chest pain, sudden breathlessness or a painful, swollen leg.
