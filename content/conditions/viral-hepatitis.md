## What it is

Hepatitis means inflammation of the liver. Several viruses cause it, and the three most common are hepatitis A, B and C. They are unrelated and spread in different ways.[^4]

| Feature | Hepatitis A | Hepatitis B | Hepatitis C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spread by | Contaminated food or water | Blood and body fluids | Blood |
| Long-term infection | No | Sometimes | Usually |
| Vaccine | Yes | Yes | No |
| Treatment | Supportive care | Long-term antivirals | Curative antivirals |

The WHO estimates that 254 million people were living with chronic hepatitis B in 2022 and about 50 million with chronic hepatitis C.[^2][^3]

## Symptoms

A new infection with any of the three can cause tiredness, fever, nausea, loss of appetite, belly pain, dark urine, pale stools and jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes).[^7] Many people have no symptoms, including most young children with hepatitis A and most people newly infected with hepatitis C.[^1][^3]

Chronic hepatitis B and C are typically silent for decades while the liver is slowly scarred. The first signs may be those of cirrhosis or liver cancer.[^2][^3]

## Causes and risk factors

- **Hepatitis A** spreads when traces of an infected person's stool get into food or water, or through close personal contact. Risk is highest where sanitation is poor.[^1]
- **Hepatitis B** spreads through blood and body fluids: from mother to baby at birth, through sex, and through shared needles or unsafe injections. Infection becomes chronic in 80% to 90% of babies infected in their first year, but in fewer than 5% of otherwise healthy adults.[^2]
- **Hepatitis C** spreads mainly through blood, by sharing injecting equipment, unsafe health care or unscreened transfusions. About 30% of people clear it within six months, and the rest develop chronic infection.[^3]

Hepatitis C is not spread by hugging, kissing or sharing food.[^3]

## Diagnosis

All three are diagnosed with blood tests. For hepatitis B, a surface antigen test shows current infection, and antibody tests show past infection or immunity.[^6] For hepatitis C, an antibody test is followed by a test for the virus's genetic material to confirm current infection.[^5]

Because chronic infection is silent, the US CDC recommends testing all adults at least once for hepatitis B and hepatitis C, and during every pregnancy.[^5][^6]

## Treatment

- **Hepatitis A** has no specific treatment. Almost everyone recovers fully within weeks to months and is then immune for life.[^1]
- **Chronic hepatitis B** is treated with antiviral tablets such as tenofovir or entecavir. They suppress the virus and lower the risk of cirrhosis and liver cancer, but they rarely cure it, so most people stay on treatment for life.[^2]
- **Hepatitis C** can be cured. Direct-acting antiviral tablets clear the virus in more than 95% of people, usually with a course of 8 to 12 weeks.[^3][^5]

## Prevention and self-care

Safe, effective vaccines prevent hepatitis A and B. The WHO recommends that every baby receive hepatitis B vaccine as soon as possible after birth, ideally within 24 hours, and the full course gives 98% to 100% protection.[^1][^2] There is no vaccine for hepatitis C.[^3] [Vaccines for Adults](/articles/adult-vaccines-guide) covers who needs which.

Wash your hands after using the toilet and before handling food, use condoms, and never share needles, razors or toothbrushes.[^1][^2] If you have hepatitis, avoid alcohol and check any medicine or supplement with your doctor first.[^4]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you develop jaundice, dark urine, or nausea and tiredness that do not pass.[^7] Ask about testing if you have never been tested.

If you may have been exposed, for example through a needle injury or an infected partner, seek advice quickly. Preventive treatment works best within 24 hours for hepatitis B and within two weeks for hepatitis A.[^4]

Call your local emergency number for confusion or unusual drowsiness, bleeding or easy bruising, vomiting blood, or jaundice with persistent vomiting. These can be signs of liver failure.[^4]
