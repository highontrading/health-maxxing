## What it is

Peripheral artery disease (PAD) is a narrowing of the arteries that carry blood to the limbs, almost always the legs. The cause is atherosclerosis, the same build-up of fatty plaque that narrows the arteries of the heart.[^1]

That shared cause is why PAD matters beyond the legs. People who have it are at higher risk of [heart attack](/conditions/heart-attack) and [stroke](/conditions/stroke), so treatment looks after the whole circulation.[^2][^3]

## Symptoms

Many people with PAD have no symptoms, or put their slower walking down to age.[^1] The classic symptom is intermittent claudication: cramping, aching or tiredness in the calf, thigh or buttock that comes on with walking and eases within minutes of rest.[^2] Other signs include:[^1][^4]

- Coldness or numbness in one foot or lower leg.
- Sores on the feet or toes that are slow to heal.
- Shiny skin, hair loss or color changes on the legs.
- Erectile dysfunction.

Pain in the feet at rest, especially at night, and wounds that will not heal are signs of severe disease.[^3]

## Causes and risk factors

Smoking and diabetes are the strongest risk factors. Others are older age, [high blood pressure](/conditions/high-blood-pressure), [high cholesterol](/conditions/high-cholesterol), chronic kidney disease and a family history of vascular disease.[^1][^3]

## Diagnosis

After feeling the pulses in your feet, a clinician measures the ankle-brachial index (ABI), which compares blood pressure at the ankle with blood pressure in the arm. A resting ABI of 0.90 or lower confirms PAD.[^3] Ultrasound, CT or MRI scans map the narrowed segments when a procedure is being considered.[^1][^3]

## Treatment

The 2024 US guideline sets out a consistent approach:[^3]

- **Stopping smoking**, the single most important step.
- **Supervised exercise therapy.** A structured walking program, typically three sessions a week for at least 12 weeks, is a first-line treatment for claudication.
- **Medicines to cut cardiovascular risk**: an antiplatelet (aspirin or clopidogrel) and a statin, plus treatment for blood pressure and diabetes. Some people are offered a low-dose anticoagulant alongside aspirin, or cilostazol to improve walking distance.
- **Procedures to restore blood flow**: angioplasty, stenting or bypass surgery, for symptoms that limit daily life despite other treatment or when the limb is under threat.

## Prevention and self-care

Do not smoke, stay active, and keep blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar in range.[^1][^2] [How to Quit Smoking (and Vaping)](/articles/how-to-quit-smoking-and-vaping) ranks the methods that work.

If you have PAD, keep walking. In an exercise program you walk until the ache builds, rest, then walk again; over weeks this lengthens the distance you can cover.[^3] Check your feet every day for cuts, blisters or color changes, particularly if you also have diabetes.[^2]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you get leg pain when walking that eases with rest, or have a sore on your foot that is not healing.[^4] Do not write it off as getting older.

**Call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department immediately** if a leg or foot suddenly becomes cold, pale or blue, numb, weak or severely painful. A suddenly blocked artery can threaten the limb within hours.[^3]
