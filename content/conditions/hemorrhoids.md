## What it is

Hemorrhoids, also called piles, are swollen veins around the anus and in the lower rectum. Internal hemorrhoids form inside the rectum. External ones form under the skin around the anus.[^1]

They are very common. About 1 in 20 Americans have them, and about half of adults over 50.[^1]

## Symptoms

Internal hemorrhoids usually cause painless bleeding: bright red blood on the toilet paper, in the bowl or on the stool. They can also push out through the anus (prolapse) and may need to be pushed back in.[^1]

External hemorrhoids cause itching, aching and tender lumps near the anus, and tend to hurt most when you sit. If a blood clot forms inside one, it becomes a hard, very painful lump.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

Anything that raises pressure in the veins of the lower rectum can contribute: straining during bowel movements, sitting on the toilet for a long time, long-term constipation or diarrhea, a low-fiber diet, pregnancy and regular heavy lifting. The tissues that support these veins also weaken with age.[^1]

## Diagnosis

A doctor can usually diagnose hemorrhoids from your symptoms and an examination, which may include a gloved finger or a short scope to look inside the rectum.[^1]

Bleeding should not simply be assumed to come from hemorrhoids. Depending on your age, symptoms and family history, your doctor may recommend a colonoscopy to rule out other causes, including [colorectal cancer](/conditions/colorectal-cancer) and inflammatory bowel disease.[^3]

## Treatment

Most hemorrhoids settle with home care, often within a few days.[^2] Guidelines put fiber and fluids first, because softer stools mean less straining.[^3] Other measures include:[^1][^2]

- A fiber supplement such as psyllium, or a stool softener.
- Not straining, and not sitting on the toilet longer than you need to.
- Sitting in a shallow bath of warm water (a sitz bath) to ease pain and itching.
- Simple pain relievers and pharmacy creams or suppositories, used as directed on the label. These are meant for short-term use, and symptoms that last more than a week should be checked.

If symptoms persist, a doctor can treat internal hemorrhoids in the clinic. The most common procedure is rubber band ligation, in which a small band cuts off the blood supply so the hemorrhoid shrinks and falls away. Injections and heat treatments are alternatives.[^3]

Surgery to remove hemorrhoids is kept for large or prolapsing ones that have not responded. A clotted external hemorrhoid can be removed under local anesthetic, which helps most when done within the first few days.[^3]

## Prevention and self-care

The aim is soft, regular stools. US dietary guidelines suggest 14 g of fiber for every 1,000 calories, or about 28 g a day on a 2,000-calorie diet, along with enough fluids.[^1] Go when you feel the urge and do not sit longer than you need to, which is easier if your phone stays outside the bathroom.[^2]

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor about any bleeding from the rectum, and if symptoms have not improved after a week of home treatment.[^1] Black, tarry stools suggest bleeding higher up in the gut and need urgent medical attention.

Go to an emergency department or call your local emergency number if bleeding is heavy or will not stop, if you pass large clots, if you are in severe pain, or if you feel dizzy or faint.[^2]
