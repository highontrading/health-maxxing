## What it is

Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition that causes marked shifts in mood, energy and activity. People have episodes of mania or its milder form, hypomania, and usually episodes of depression, often with stretches of stable mood in between.[^2]

The two main types differ in how severe the highs are.[^2] In bipolar I disorder, a person has had at least one manic episode lasting a week or more, or severe enough to need hospital care. In bipolar II disorder, depressive episodes alternate with hypomania, without full mania.

Roughly 1 in every 200 people worldwide lives with bipolar disorder. It affects men and women at similar rates and usually begins in the late teens or early adulthood.[^1][^2]

## Symptoms

During a **manic episode**, a person may feel elated, wired or unusually irritable, need far less sleep, talk fast, have racing thoughts, make grand plans and act in risky ways, such as overspending or reckless driving. Severe mania can include psychosis: hallucinations or fixed false beliefs.[^2]

**Hypomania** is similar but milder. It may even feel productive, which is one reason it often goes unreported.[^2]

During a **depressive episode**, the picture is that of [depression](/conditions/depression): low mood, loss of interest, exhaustion, changes in sleep and appetite, and sometimes thoughts of suicide. Some episodes mix symptoms of both kinds.[^2]

## Causes and risk factors

No single cause is known. Genes play a large part: bipolar disorder often runs in families, although most relatives of someone with the condition never develop it.[^2] Episodes can be set off by major stress, disrupted sleep, and alcohol or drug use.[^1][^2]

## Diagnosis

A psychiatrist or other mental health specialist makes the diagnosis from a detailed history of your moods, energy, sleep and behavior over time, ideally with input from someone who knows you well.[^3] No blood test or scan can diagnose it, though tests may be done to rule out other causes of the symptoms.[^5]

Diagnosis is often delayed. People usually seek help when they are depressed, and earlier highs may not be mentioned, so the condition can be mistaken for depression alone.[^2]

## Treatment

Bipolar disorder needs long-term treatment, usually medication combined with psychological support.[^2]

- **Mood stabilizers.** NICE recommends lithium as the first-choice long-term treatment. It requires regular blood tests to check the lithium level and your kidney and thyroid function.[^3]
- **Other medicines** include certain antipsychotics and the anti-seizure medicines valproate and lamotrigine.[^2][^3] Valproate can seriously harm an unborn baby, so its use is restricted in women and girls who could become pregnant.[^3]
- **Psychological therapies**, such as psychoeducation, cognitive behavioral therapy and family-focused therapy, help you spot early warning signs and prevent relapse.[^2][^3]

Antidepressants are used cautiously, because on their own they can trigger mania.[^3] Do not stop medication suddenly without talking to your prescriber. Symptoms can return or worsen.[^2]

## Prevention and self-care

There is no known way to prevent bipolar disorder, but relapses can often be headed off. What helps: taking medication consistently, keeping regular sleep and daily routines, avoiding alcohol and drugs, tracking your mood, and agreeing a plan with your care team and family for what to do when early warning signs appear.[^1][^2] These steps work alongside treatment, not instead of it.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if you have had periods of unusually high energy, little need for sleep or out-of-character risk-taking, especially if you have also been depressed.

If you take lithium, get same-day medical advice for vomiting, diarrhea, a severe tremor, unsteadiness or confusion, which can mean the level in your blood is too high.[^3]

The risk of suicide is higher in people with bipolar disorder.[^1] If you are thinking about harming yourself, or someone is in danger during an episode, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line straight away. Find A Helpline lists free, confidential services by country.[^4]
