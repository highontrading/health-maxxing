## What it is

Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a blood clot in a deep vein, usually in the calf or thigh. It matters because part of the clot can break off, travel through the bloodstream and lodge in the lungs. That is a pulmonary embolism (PE), and it can be fatal. Together, DVT and PE are called venous thromboembolism.[^2]

**A suspected DVT needs medical assessment the same day. Signs of a pulmonary embolism need an emergency call.**

## Symptoms

DVT usually affects one leg:[^1]

- Throbbing or cramping pain, usually in the calf or thigh, often when walking or standing.
- Swelling.
- Warm skin around the painful area.
- Red or darkened skin, which may be harder to see on brown or black skin.
- Swollen veins that are hard or sore to touch.

Some clots cause no leg symptoms, and the first sign is a PE: sudden breathlessness, chest pain that is worse when you breathe in, or coughing up blood.[^1][^2]

## Causes and risk factors

Clots form when blood flow slows, a vein wall is damaged or the blood clots more readily than usual. The risk rises with:[^2][^3]

- Recent surgery, a hospital stay or a leg injury.
- Long periods of not moving, such as bed rest or long journeys.
- Cancer and some cancer treatments.
- Pregnancy and the weeks after giving birth.
- Contraceptives or hormone therapy that contain estrogen.
- A previous clot, a family history of clots or an inherited clotting disorder.
- Older age, obesity and smoking.

## Diagnosis

Doctors first judge how likely a clot is from your symptoms and risk factors. A D-dimer blood test can help rule out a clot when the likelihood is low. The main test is an ultrasound scan of the leg veins.[^1][^2]

## Treatment

Anticoagulant medicines ("blood thinners") are the main treatment. They do not dissolve the clot. They stop it from growing and prevent new clots while your body breaks the old one down.[^2]

Guidelines from the American College of Chest Physicians (2021) and the American Society of Hematology (2020) favor direct oral anticoagulants, such as apixaban or rivaroxaban, over warfarin for most people, with treatment lasting at least three months.[^4][^5] Some people need longer or indefinite treatment, depending on what caused the clot and their risk of bleeding.[^4]

Injected heparin is used in some situations, including pregnancy. Clot-dissolving drugs and procedures to remove the clot are reserved for severe cases, and a filter placed in a large vein is an option when anticoagulants cannot be used.[^2][^5]

## Prevention and self-care

- After surgery or illness, get moving as soon as your care team advises, and use any preventive medicine or compression devices you are given.[^2]
- On long journeys, walk around when you can, flex your calves while seated and drink water.[^1]
- Stay active, keep a healthy weight and do not smoke.[^1]
- If you take an anticoagulant, take it exactly as prescribed and report any unusual bleeding.[^6]

## When to see a doctor

Get medical advice the same day if you have pain and swelling in one leg, especially after surgery, a long journey or a period of immobility.[^1]

**Call your local emergency number immediately** if you have sudden breathlessness, sharp chest pain, a racing heartbeat, fainting, or you cough up blood, with or without leg symptoms. These suggest a pulmonary embolism.[^1][^6]
