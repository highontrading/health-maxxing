## What it is

Blood pressure is the force of blood against the walls of your arteries. It is written as two numbers: systolic pressure (when the heart beats) over diastolic pressure (when it rests between beats), measured in millimeters of mercury (mm Hg).

Hypertension means that force stays too high over time, which damages blood vessels and makes the heart work harder. The WHO defines it as 140/90 mm Hg or higher on two different days.[^1] US guidelines use a lower threshold, classing 130/80 mm Hg or higher as stage 1 hypertension.[^2] An estimated 1.4 billion adults aged 30 to 79 had hypertension in 2024, and about 44% of them did not know it.[^1]

## Symptoms

Most people feel nothing, which is why it is called a silent killer. The only way to know is to measure.[^1] Very high pressure can cause headaches, blurred vision, nosebleeds, chest pain or shortness of breath, but waiting for symptoms means waiting too long.[^1]

## Causes and risk factors

For most adults there is no single cause. Risk rises with age, a family history of high blood pressure, excess weight, a diet high in salt and low in potassium, physical inactivity, regular heavy drinking and tobacco use.[^1][^4] In a minority of people another condition is responsible, such as kidney disease, sleep apnea, thyroid or adrenal problems, or certain medicines.[^4]

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on repeated readings, not one. A health worker will measure after you have sat quietly for a few minutes, with your arm supported at heart level, and will usually confirm high readings with home or 24-hour monitoring.[^2] The US categories are:[^3]

| Category | Systolic | | Diastolic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Below 120 | and | Below 80 |
| Elevated | 120 to 129 | and | Below 80 |
| Stage 1 | 130 to 139 | or | 80 to 89 |
| Stage 2 | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher |

Our [blood pressure checker](/tools/blood-pressure) shows where a reading falls, and [Blood Pressure Numbers, Explained](/articles/blood-pressure-numbers-explained) covers how to measure properly at home.

## Treatment

Treatment combines lifestyle changes with medication when needed. Doctors decide based on your readings and your overall cardiovascular risk.[^2] Commonly used medicines include ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, calcium channel blockers and thiazide-type diuretics, and many people need more than one.[^2] Treatment is usually long term, and stopping tablets because you feel fine is a common reason pressure climbs again.

## Prevention and self-care

The same habits prevent hypertension and help treat it: eating more vegetables and fruit and less salt, staying physically active, keeping a healthy weight, limiting alcohol and not smoking.[^1] See [the DASH diet and salt](/articles/dash-diet-and-salt) for the eating pattern with the best evidence. Home monitoring helps you and your doctor see whether changes are working.[^3]

## When to see a doctor

Have your blood pressure checked at least every few years as an adult, and more often if it has been raised before. Book an appointment if home readings are consistently 130/80 mm Hg or higher.

A reading above 180/120 mm Hg needs prompt medical advice. If it comes with chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness or weakness, vision changes or difficulty speaking, call your local emergency number.[^3]
