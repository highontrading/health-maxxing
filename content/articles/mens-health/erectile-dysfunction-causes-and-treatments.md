Erection problems are among the most common health complaints men have, and among the least discussed. That silence has a cost. Erectile dysfunction (ED) is usually treatable, and in some men it is the earliest visible sign of disease in the blood vessels, arriving years before any chest pain.

This guide covers how common ED is, what causes it, what it can tell you about your heart, and the treatments, from exercise to tablets to devices, that have evidence behind them.

## How common is it?

ED means being unable to get or keep an erection firm enough for sex. The occasional off night is normal. A problem that keeps happening, or keeps getting worse, is not, and it deserves attention.[^1]

> [!STAT] 52%
> The share of men aged 40 to 70 who reported some degree of erectile dysfunction in the Massachusetts Male Aging Study, a landmark survey of 1,290 men.[^2]

In that study, the share of men with complete ED tripled between the ages of 40 and 70, from 5% to 15%.[^2] The US National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) estimates that about 30 million American men are affected.[^1]

## What causes it

An erection is a blood-flow event. Arousal sends nerve signals that relax the muscle in the arteries and spongy tissue of the penis, blood flows in, and the swelling traps it there. Anything that interferes with the nerves, the blood vessels, the hormones or the mind can interrupt the sequence.[^10]

| Cause | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Blood vessels | Atherosclerosis, high blood pressure, diabetes, smoking |
| Nerves | Diabetes, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury, prostate or pelvic surgery and radiotherapy |
| Hormones | Low testosterone |
| Medicines | Some blood pressure medicines, antidepressants, antiandrogens and sedatives |
| Mind | Fear of failure, stress, anxiety, depression |
| Lifestyle | Heavy drinking, drug use, excess weight, inactivity |

Most cases have more than one cause.[^3][^10] Physical and psychological factors also feed each other: one failure breeds anxiety, and anxiety makes the next failure more likely. One clue that the mind is the main driver is a problem that comes and goes, for example when you still wake with erections but cannot get one during sex.[^8]

## The heart link

The arteries that supply the penis are narrower than those that supply the heart, so the same process of artery damage tends to show itself there first. An expert consensus panel concluded that ED typically appears two to five years before a cardiac event, which makes it a window for prevention.[^4]

The numbers bear this out. A meta-analysis of 12 prospective studies covering more than 36,000 men found that men with ED had a 48% higher risk of cardiovascular disease, a 46% higher risk of coronary heart disease and a 35% higher risk of stroke than men without it.[^5] These were observational studies, so ED is best seen as a marker of shared artery disease, not a cause of heart attacks. The American Urological Association (AUA) guideline tells doctors to inform men that ED is a risk marker for cardiovascular disease.[^3]

Diabetes deserves a mention of its own: men with diabetes are two to three times more likely to have ED than men without it.[^10]

> [!TIP] Use ED as a prompt
> If you have erection problems, especially before 60 and without an obvious explanation, ask for your blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar to be checked. Our guide to [heart disease risk factors](/articles/heart-disease-risk-factors) explains what the results mean.

## Lifestyle changes with evidence

- **Weight loss and activity.** In a randomized trial of 110 obese men with ED, those given intensive support to lose weight and exercise more were far more likely to regain normal erectile function after two years: 31%, against 5% in the comparison group.[^6]
- **Aerobic exercise.** A systematic review concluded that 40 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise four times a week for six months improves erectile function in men whose ED is linked to inactivity, obesity, high blood pressure or cardiovascular disease.[^7]
- **Smoking and alcohol.** NIDDK's advice also includes stopping smoking and limiting alcohol.[^9] Our guide to [quitting smoking](/articles/how-to-quit-smoking-and-vaping) can help.

## Treatments that work

### Tablets

PDE5 inhibitors are the usual first medicine: sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil and avanafil. They relax the smooth muscle of the penis so that more blood flows in when you are aroused. They do not produce an erection by themselves, so you still need sexual stimulation.[^9]

About 7 in 10 men get better erections with them, although response rates are lower in men with diabetes and after cancer treatment. Common side effects include headache, flushing, a blocked nose, indigestion and muscle aches.[^11]

> [!WARNING]
> Never combine PDE5 inhibitors with nitrates, such as nitroglycerin (glyceryl trinitrate) spray or tablets for angina, or with recreational "poppers". The combination can cause a sudden, dangerous drop in blood pressure.[^9] If you are ever treated for chest pain, tell the team that you have taken one.

### When tablets are not enough

The AUA guideline says men should be told about every option that is suitable for them, not marched through a fixed sequence.[^3] The options include:[^3][^9]

- a vacuum device, which draws blood into the penis with a pump and holds it there with a ring
- alprostadil, given as a pellet placed in the urethra or an injection into the side of the penis
- a penile implant, placed surgically
- testosterone, but only when blood tests confirm a deficiency (see our [testosterone explainer](/articles/testosterone-explained))
- counseling or sex therapy, alone or with a partner, which the AUA recommends alongside physical treatments because anxiety and relationship strain affect how well any of them works

Be cautious with clinics that sell shockwave therapy, stem cells or platelet-rich plasma injections. The AUA classes the first two as investigational and the third as experimental.[^3]

## Buying safely

Erection medicines are among the most counterfeited drugs in the world, and counterfeits have been found with the wrong dose, the wrong drug or contaminants.[^12] "Natural" enhancement products are not necessarily safe either. NIDDK warns that some combinations with prescribed medicines can cause serious problems, and that you should never order a medicine online without talking to a doctor.[^9]

In the UK, sildenafil can be bought from a pharmacist without a prescription.[^8] Wherever you live, use a licensed pharmacy.

## Mind, mood and relationships

ED is tangled up with mood in both directions. Depression and anxiety can cause it, and living with it can leave men feeling low, embarrassed or avoidant of intimacy.[^10] Talking to a partner tends to take pressure off, and a few sessions with a therapist can break the cycle of performance anxiety. Our piece on [men's mental health](/articles/mens-mental-health) has ideas for starting that conversation.

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor if erection problems keep happening.[^8] It is a routine consultation, and the checks may uncover high blood pressure, diabetes or low testosterone. Get emergency care for an erection that lasts more than four hours, or for chest pain during sex. If ED began after you started a new medicine, do not stop it on your own; ask about alternatives.

## The bottom line

[Erectile dysfunction](/conditions/erectile-dysfunction) is common, it is rarely "just age", and it is usually treatable. Treat it as a reason to check your heart risk, give exercise and weight loss a serious try, and know that tablets work for most men as long as you are not taking nitrates. Buy from a licensed pharmacy, and if tablets fail, there are effective next steps.
