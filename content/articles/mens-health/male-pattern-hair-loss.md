If your hairline is retreating or your crown is starting to show, you are in very large company. More than half of men over 50 have some degree of pattern hair loss.[^1] You are also the target market for a vast industry of shampoos, gummies, lasers and clinics, most of which promise more than they can demonstrate.

The evidence is fairly clear. Two medicines have decades of trial data, a few newer options have some, and most of the rest has little or none. This guide sorts them, is honest about side effects, and covers the signs that your hair loss is something other than male pattern baldness.

## Why it happens

Male pattern hair loss, or androgenetic alopecia, follows a recognizable route. The hairline recedes at the temples into an M shape, the crown thins, and the two areas may eventually meet.[^1]

It takes two ingredients: genes and androgens. In men who are genetically susceptible, the follicles on top of the scalp are unusually sensitive to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a potent hormone made from testosterone. Over years, each hair's growth phase gets shorter and the follicle shrinks, so hairs come back finer and shorter until they stop appearing.[^1][^5] Follicles on the back and sides resist the process, which is why that hair survives and why it can be transplanted.

The genetics are complicated. Many genes are involved and the condition clusters in families, so the old rule about your mother's father is only part of the story.[^1]

> The share of men over 50 who have some degree of male pattern hair loss. It can begin as early as the teens.[^1]

## The two treatments with the best evidence

Treatments are better at holding on to hair than at bringing it back, so they work best when started early.[^5]

### Minoxidil on the scalp

Minoxidil is a liquid or foam that you apply to the scalp, and in many countries you can buy it without a prescription. In a 48-week randomized trial of 393 men, the 5% solution produced 45% more regrowth than the 2% solution, and both beat placebo. The main side effect was an itchy, irritated scalp.[^4]

Use it every day. A temporary increase in shedding in the first weeks is common, so judge the result at six months, not six weeks.[^5] It only works for as long as you keep using it.[^2]

### Finasteride tablets

Finasteride blocks the enzyme that converts testosterone into DHT. In the pivotal trials, which enrolled 1,553 men aged 18 to 41, 83% of those taking it had no further hair loss after two years, compared with 28% on placebo. Two thirds were judged to have visibly improved, against 7% on placebo.[^3]

The side effects deserve a clear-eyed look:

- **Sexual side effects.** A lower sex drive and erection or ejaculation problems were reported by 3.8% of men on finasteride and 2.1% on placebo in the first year of those trials.[^3]
- **Mood.** The NHS lists depression and thoughts of self-harm among the possible serious side effects, and advises stopping the medicine and speaking to a doctor if they occur.[^9]
- **Persistent symptoms.** Some men report sexual or mood symptoms that continue after stopping. How often this happens, and why, is still debated.

Two practical points. Finasteride lowers PSA, the blood marker used in [prostate testing](/articles/prostate-health-guide), so tell any doctor who orders that test. And women who are or could become pregnant should not handle crushed or broken tablets.[^9]

> [!WARNING]
> If you notice low mood, anxiety or thoughts of harming yourself while taking finasteride, stop taking it and contact a doctor promptly.[^9] If you feel at risk of acting on those thoughts, call your local emergency number or a crisis line.

## Newer and off-label options

- **Low-dose oral minoxidil.** Minoxidil began life as a blood pressure tablet, and low doses are now widely prescribed off-label for hair loss. In a 24-week randomized trial in men, the tablet was not clearly better than the 5% scalp solution, and about half of those taking it grew extra body or facial hair.[^7] In a safety review of 1,404 patients, 15% developed excess hair, fewer than 2% had lightheadedness, fluid retention or a fast heartbeat, and 1.7% stopped because of side effects.[^8] It needs a prescriber who knows your heart history.
- **Dutasteride.** This stronger relative of finasteride is licensed for hair loss in only a few countries and used off-label elsewhere. In a network meta-analysis of 23 trials, it ranked highest for increasing hair count at 24 weeks, ahead of finasteride and minoxidil.[^6] Expect a similar range of side effects.

## Options with thinner evidence

| Option | What the evidence looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Low-level light therapy (laser combs and caps) | European guidelines say it can be considered; trials are small and benefits modest |
| Microneedling | In a 12-week pilot trial of 100 men, adding weekly microneedling to minoxidil increased hair counts far more than minoxidil alone, but it was one small, short study |
| Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections | Some positive small trials; methods vary widely and the evidence is limited |

Those summaries draw on the European evidence-based guideline and the microneedling trial itself.[^5][^11]

## Hair transplants

A transplant moves DHT-resistant follicles from the back and sides of the head to the thinning areas. In a suitable candidate it gives lasting results for the transplanted hairs. It does not stop the surrounding hair from thinning, which is why guidelines suggest combining surgery with medical treatment.[^5]

It is still surgery, it is usually paid for privately, and results depend heavily on the surgeon.[^2] Check qualifications, and be wary of prices that look too good.

## What does not work

Biotin is the best-known hair supplement, and there is no good evidence that it helps people who are not deficient. Deficiency is rare. High doses can also distort blood tests, including some used to detect heart attacks and thyroid disease.[^10] Our guide to [collagen, biotin and beauty supplements](/articles/collagen-biotin-beauty-supplements) goes through the rest of that shelf.

## When it might be something else

Male pattern hair loss is gradual and follows the classic pattern. Other causes, including stress, illness, some medicines, thyroid disease and scalp infections, look different.[^12]

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor if your hair loss is sudden, if hair is coming out in clumps or leaving round bald patches, or if your scalp itches, burns or is scaly.[^2] These patterns point to causes that need their own treatment.

Shedding all over the scalp a few months after an illness, a crash diet or a stressful event is usually temporary. Our guide to [hair shedding and thinning](/articles/hair-shedding-and-thinning) explains the other patterns.

> [!NOTE]
> Doing nothing is a legitimate choice. Hair loss is not an illness, and plenty of men shave it off and move on. If it is affecting your confidence or mood, though, that is worth saying out loud to your doctor.[^2]

## The bottom line

If you want to keep your hair, start early and choose treatments with evidence: minoxidil on the scalp and finasteride tablets lead the field, with low-dose oral minoxidil and dutasteride as off-label alternatives under a prescriber's eye. Give any treatment six months, go into finasteride with open eyes about its side effects, and save your money on biotin. If hair loss is sudden, patchy or comes with scalp symptoms, get it checked.
