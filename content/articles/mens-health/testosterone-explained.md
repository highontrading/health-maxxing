Few hormones carry as much cultural baggage as testosterone. It is sold as the explanation for tiredness, a spreading waistline and a flat mood, and as the cure for all three. Use of prescription testosterone among US men aged 40 and over more than tripled between 2001 and 2011, and about a quarter of the men who started it had not had their level tested in the previous year.[^12]

The medical picture is narrower and more useful. This explainer covers what testosterone does, what counts as low, what pushes levels down, and what testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) has and has not been shown to do.

## What testosterone does

In men, testosterone is made mainly in the testicles, on instruction from the pituitary gland in the brain. It drives the changes of puberty, and in adults it supports sex drive, sperm production, muscle mass and bone strength.[^11]

Levels follow a daily rhythm and peak in the morning, which is why blood tests are taken early.[^2] They also drift down with age. The NHS puts the decline at less than 2% a year from around the age of 30 to 40 and notes that this gradual fall is unlikely to cause problems by itself. There is no male version of menopause, in which hormone levels drop sharply over a few years.[^6]

## What counts as low?

A low number alone is not a diagnosis. Both major US guidelines require two things together: symptoms or signs of testosterone deficiency, and consistently low levels on blood tests.[^1][^2]

- **The American Urological Association (AUA)** uses a total testosterone below 300 ng/dL (10.4 nmol/L), measured on two separate early mornings.[^1]
- **The Endocrine Society** asks for fasting morning tests that show clearly low levels, confirmed by repeating them. It cites a normal range of roughly 264 to 916 ng/dL (9.2 to 31.8 nmol/L) in healthy young men.[^2]

One low result is not enough, because levels vary from day to day and dip with illness and after meals. A sizeable minority of men with one low reading turn out to be normal when the test is repeated.[^2]

The symptoms most specific to low testosterone are sexual: reduced sex drive, fewer morning erections and erectile difficulties. In a study of more than 3,000 European men aged 40 to 79, those three symptoms combined with low blood levels defined genuine late-onset low testosterone.[^5] Tiredness, low mood and poor concentration are common too, but they have many other causes.

> [!STAT] 2.1%
> The share of men aged 40 to 79 who met strict criteria for late-onset low testosterone (sexual symptoms plus low blood levels) in a large European study.[^5]

## Why levels fall

Low testosterone comes from a problem in the testicles themselves or in the brain signals that drive them. Causes include genetic conditions, injury, infection or cancer treatment affecting the testicles, pituitary disorders, and medicines such as opioids and steroid tablets. Obesity, type 2 diabetes and other long-term illness lower levels too.[^2]

Some of this is reversible:

- **Weight.** A meta-analysis found that losing weight raises testosterone, and the more weight men lost, the bigger the rise.[^7]
- **Sleep.** In a small laboratory study, a week of sleeping five hours a night cut young men's daytime testosterone by 10% to 15%.[^8] Guidelines also advise against starting TRT while severe [sleep apnea](/articles/snoring-and-sleep-apnea) is untreated.[^2]
- **Medicines and alcohol.** If you take a medicine known to lower testosterone, ask whether there is an alternative before adding a hormone on top.

## What TRT can and cannot do

TRT comes as gels, injections, patches and pellets placed under the skin. The aim is to bring levels into the middle of the normal range, which the AUA defines as 450 to 600 ng/dL, not to push them as high as possible.[^1]

| Outcome | What the evidence shows |
| --- | --- |
| Sex drive and sexual activity | Modest improvement |
| Erections, anemia, bone density, lean body mass, low mood | May improve |
| Energy, fatigue, thinking skills, quality of life | Inconclusive |
| Fractures | Not reduced: more fractures on testosterone in the largest trial |
| Heart attack and stroke | No increase in the largest trial, but more atrial fibrillation and lung clots |

The best evidence in older men comes from the Testosterone Trials, in which 790 men aged 65 or over with low levels used testosterone gel or a placebo for a year. Sexual desire and activity improved modestly. Vitality and walking distance barely moved.[^4]

The AUA's summary is that treatment may improve sex drive, erectile function, anemia, bone density, lean body mass and depressive symptoms, while the evidence is inconclusive for energy, fatigue, cognition, diabetes measures and quality of life.[^1]

## The risks

**Heart and circulation.** For years the main worry was heart attacks and strokes. The TRAVERSE trial tested this directly in 5,246 men aged 45 to 80 who had low testosterone and either heart disease or a high risk of it. Major cardiac events occurred in 7.0% of men on testosterone gel and 7.3% on placebo. However, atrial fibrillation (3.5% vs 2.4%), blood clots in the lungs (0.9% vs 0.5%) and acute kidney injury (2.3% vs 1.5%) were more common with testosterone.[^3]

**Fractures.** Testosterone raises bone density, so fewer fractures were expected. TRAVERSE found the opposite: about 3.5% of men on testosterone had a fracture, compared with about 2.5% on placebo.[^9]

**Fertility.** This is the risk men are least often told about. Testosterone from outside the body switches off the brain signals that drive the testicles, so sperm production falls and can stop altogether. The AUA says testosterone should not be prescribed to men who are trying to conceive.[^1] Our guide to [male fertility](/articles/male-fertility-basics) explains the alternatives.

**Thicker blood.** Testosterone stimulates red blood cell production. Doctors check your hematocrit, the share of your blood made up of red cells, before and during treatment, and the AUA advises stepping in if it reaches 54%.[^1]

**Who should not start.** The Endocrine Society advises against TRT for men with breast or prostate cancer, a raised hematocrit, untreated severe sleep apnea, uncontrolled heart failure, or a heart attack or stroke in the past six months, and for men planning to have children soon.[^2] Gels can also transfer to partners and children through skin contact, so wash your hands and cover the area.[^1]

> [!WARNING]
> Do not start testosterone on the strength of symptoms, a questionnaire or a single blood test, and never from an unregulated source. The doses used in bodybuilding are far above replacement doses and carry different, larger risks.

## What about boosters?

Supplements sold as testosterone boosters are a different thing from prescribed testosterone, and the evidence behind them is thin. A review of 50 popular products found that fewer than a quarter of their ingredients had any published data showing they raise testosterone, and most had no data at all.[^10] Sleep and a healthy weight will do more than a capsule.

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor if you have a persistently low sex drive, [erection problems](/articles/erectile-dysfunction-causes-and-treatments), loss of body hair, breast swelling or unexplained fatigue, and ask for morning blood tests. If you are on TRT, get urgent help for chest pain, sudden breathlessness, a painful swollen calf or a racing, irregular heartbeat.

## The bottom line

[Low testosterone](/conditions/low-testosterone) is real, but it is diagnosed with symptoms plus two low morning tests, not with a quiz. Before treatment, look for reversible causes such as weight, sleep and medicines. If you do start TRT, expect a modest improvement in sex drive and little else guaranteed, protect any plans for children, and keep up the blood tests.
