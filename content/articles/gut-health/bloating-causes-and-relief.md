Your waistband fits at breakfast and digs in by dinner. That tight, overfull feeling is one of the most common gut complaints there is: in a global survey, close to one in five adults said they felt bloated at least once a week.[^3]

Most of the time bloating is uncomfortable, not dangerous, and it comes down to a short list of causes you can test for yourself. This guide covers what is going on, which fixes have evidence behind them, and the signs that mean you should see a doctor instead of adjusting your diet.

## Bloating and distension are not the same thing

Doctors separate two things that often travel together. **Bloating** is a sensation: pressure, fullness or trapped gas. **Distension** is a measurable increase in the size of your abdomen. Some people feel very bloated with no visible change, and others see their belly expand without much discomfort.[^1]

The difference matters because the causes, and so the fixes, are not always the same.

## The usual suspects

| Cause | Clues | First thing to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constipation | Hard or infrequent stools, straining | Treat the constipation |
| Swallowed air | Fast eating, gum, fizzy drinks, smoking | Slow down, cut gum and fizz |
| Fermentable carbs (FODMAPs) | Worse after onions, garlic, beans, wheat, some fruit, sugar-free sweets | Short, targeted food trial |
| Lactose | Worse after milk or ice cream | Two weeks with less lactose |
| Rapid fiber increase | Started a new high-fiber diet or supplement | Step back, then build up slowly |
| Menstrual cycle | Peaks in the days before a period | Track it; it usually passes |
| IBS | Bloating with recurrent pain and altered bowel habits | See the IBS guide below |

Constipation is a frequent culprit: stool that sits in the colon gives bacteria longer to ferment it, and a full bowel slows the passage of gas.[^1] Swallowed air builds up when you eat quickly, chew gum, suck sweets, smoke or drink carbonated drinks.[^4] Certain carbohydrates are poorly absorbed and get fermented by gut bacteria, including the sugar alcohols in sugar-free gum and the sugars in beans, onions and some fruits.[^4] Bloating is also a recognized part of premenstrual syndrome.[^6]

Less often, bloating is a sign of something else, such as [celiac disease](/conditions/celiac-disease), and doctors may test for it when symptoms persist.[^1]

## It is often not "too much gas"

Here is the surprising part. Many people with troublesome bloating do not produce more gas than anyone else. Two other things are going on.[^1][^2]

- **A more sensitive gut.** In irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and related conditions, the nerves of the gut over-report normal stretching, so an ordinary amount of gas feels like a lot.
- **A muscle reflex that works backwards.** Normally, when the gut fills, the diaphragm relaxes upward and the abdominal wall tightens. In some people the diaphragm pushes down and the abdominal wall relaxes instead, so the belly visibly balloons. Specialists treat this with breathing retraining and biofeedback.

That is why products aimed purely at "reducing gas" often disappoint.

## What actually helps

### Fix constipation first

If you are not emptying your bowels comfortably and regularly, start there. Relief of constipation often brings relief of bloating.[^1] Our [constipation guide](/articles/constipation-relief-that-works) ranks the options.

### Change how you eat before what you eat

The NHS advice is simple: eat smaller, more frequent meals instead of large ones, chew with your mouth closed, and go easy on fizzy drinks, alcohol and large late-night meals.[^5] Slowing down and dropping gum cuts the amount of air you swallow.[^4]

### Move

Regular exercise helps digestion and can prevent bloating.[^5] In a small laboratory study of people with bloating, gentle pedaling helped gas move through the gut and eased symptoms compared with resting.[^7] A 10 to 15 minute walk after meals is an easy way to test this on yourself.

### Run a short, targeted food trial

If a food seems to be the trigger, the simplest test is to cut it down for a couple of weeks and see what happens, then bring it back to confirm.[^1] Good candidates are lactose, sugar-free products and the foods in the table above. If you are unsure whether you are dealing with an intolerance or something more serious, see [food intolerance vs. food allergy](/articles/food-intolerance-vs-food-allergy).

A full low-FODMAP diet can reduce bloating in people with IBS, but it is restrictive. Expert guidance says it should be done with a dietitian, for a limited time, and followed by reintroducing foods.[^1] See [IBS and the low-FODMAP diet](/articles/ibs-and-the-low-fodmap-diet).

> [!TIP] Keep a two-week diary
> Note what and when you eat, your bowel movements, your cycle if you have one, and when the bloating peaks. A pattern usually shows up, and it gives a doctor or dietitian something concrete to work with.

### Peppermint oil, if IBS is behind it

The American College of Gastroenterology suggests peppermint oil for overall IBS symptoms, though it rates the recommendation as conditional and the evidence as low quality.[^8] Enteric-coated capsules are the form used in most trials. Heartburn is a possible side effect.[^9]

### Over-the-counter remedies

Simethicone, lactase tablets (for lactose) and alpha-galactosidase, an enzyme taken with beans and vegetables, are widely available and can be used as directed on the label.[^4] Evidence for simethicone and activated charcoal is limited and conflicting, so judge them by whether they help you.[^2]

## What does not help

**"Detox" and "flat tummy" teas.** There is no convincing evidence that detox or cleansing products do what they claim or improve health. Some programs rely on laxatives, which can cause diarrhea severe enough to lead to dehydration and electrolyte problems.[^10] Any flatter stomach is short-lived.

**Probiotics.** The American Gastroenterological Association's expert review advises against using probiotics to treat bloating and distension.[^1]

**Long lists of banned foods.** Cutting many foods indefinitely risks a poor diet and anxious eating without solving the problem. Restriction should be brief and followed by reintroduction.[^1]

> [!WARNING]
> Be wary of any product that promises a flatter stomach in days. US regulators have taken action against cleansing products that contained illegal, potentially harmful ingredients or made false health claims.[^10]

## When bloating needs a doctor

Occasional bloating that comes and goes with meals or your cycle is rarely serious. Persistent bloating is different.

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor if you have felt bloated for three weeks or more, feel bloated regularly (more than about 12 times a month), have a swelling or lump in your abdomen, or have bloating along with vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, weight loss or blood in your stool.[^5] Get urgent help for sudden or severe abdominal pain, vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, or being unable to pass stool or gas.[^5]

One cause deserves special mention. Ovarian cancer often shows itself through vague symptoms: persistent bloating, feeling full quickly, pelvic or abdominal pain, and needing to urinate more often.[^11] UK guidance from the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) says women with these symptoms on a persistent or frequent basis, particularly more than 12 times a month and especially at age 50 or over, should be offered a blood test.[^12] Most women with bloating do not have cancer, but new, constant bloating after 50 should not be put down to diet without a check.

## The bottom line

Start with the basics: treat constipation, slow your eating, move after meals and test one suspected food at a time. Skip the teas and the probiotics. And if bloating is persistent, frequent or comes with weight loss, bleeding or a lump, see a doctor before changing anything else.
