Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) is common, real and frustrating. Scans and blood tests come back normal while your gut does anything but behave normally. In a large international survey using current diagnostic criteria, about 4% of adults met the definition; older, looser criteria put the figure nearer 10%.[^2]

The low-FODMAP diet is the best-studied dietary treatment, and it is frequently done wrong: too strict, for too long, without the reintroduction that makes it useful. This guide explains how IBS is diagnosed, how the diet is meant to work and what else is worth trying.

## What IBS is and how it is diagnosed

IBS is diagnosed from a pattern of symptoms. Under the Rome IV criteria, used worldwide, that means recurrent abdominal pain on average at least one day a week over the last three months, with two or more of the following:[^1]

- the pain is related to bowel movements
- it comes with a change in how often you go
- it comes with a change in the form of your stool

Symptoms should have started at least six months earlier.[^1] Doctors then classify IBS by the dominant stool pattern: constipation (IBS-C), diarrhea (IBS-D), mixed (IBS-M) or unclassified.[^1] [Bloating](/articles/bloating-causes-and-relief) is very common too.[^4]

The American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) recommends a "positive" diagnosis, made from typical symptoms plus a few targeted tests, in place of a long hunt to exclude everything else. For people with diarrhea, that means a blood test for [celiac disease](/conditions/celiac-disease) and markers that help rule out inflammatory bowel disease. It suggests against routine colonoscopy for people under 45 with no warning signs, and against food allergy or sensitivity testing.[^3]

## A gut-brain condition

IBS is now classed as a disorder of gut-brain interaction. The gut's nerves are more sensitive, its muscle contractions can be faster or slower than usual, and the signals passing between gut and brain are altered.[^4] That is why stress can set off symptoms, why IBS sometimes starts after a gut infection, and why treatments aimed at the brain-gut connection can help.[^4]

None of this means the symptoms are imagined. It means the wiring is over-responsive.

## First steps before any special diet

UK guidance puts simple measures first:[^5][^6]

- eat regular meals and take your time over them
- drink enough fluid, and cut back on coffee, tea, alcohol and fizzy drinks
- go easy on insoluble fiber such as wheat bran, which can make pain and bloating worse
- take regular exercise

If you need more fiber, choose a soluble type such as psyllium (ispaghula). The ACG recommends soluble, not insoluble, fiber for overall IBS symptoms.[^3]

## What FODMAPs are

FODMAP stands for fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides and polyols: a family of short-chain carbohydrates that are poorly absorbed in the small intestine. They draw water into the gut and are fermented by bacteria in the colon, producing gas. In a sensitive gut, that stretch triggers pain, bloating and changes in bowel habit.[^7]

| Group | The carbohydrate | Common sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oligosaccharides | Fructans, GOS | Wheat, rye, onions, garlic, beans, lentils |
| Disaccharides | Lactose | Milk, yogurt, soft cheese |
| Monosaccharides | Excess fructose | Honey, apples, mango |
| Polyols | Sorbitol, mannitol | Stone fruits, mushrooms, cauliflower, sugar-free sweets |

FODMAPs are not harmful. Many are prebiotic fibers that feed useful gut bacteria, which is one reason the strict phase is temporary.[^9]

## The three phases

The diet was developed at Monash University in Australia and has three steps.[^8]

1. **Restrict (2 to 6 weeks).** Swap high-FODMAP foods for low-FODMAP alternatives: for example, oats or rice in place of wheat, lactose-free milk in place of regular milk, and oranges or grapes in place of apples. If symptoms have not improved by the end, FODMAPs are probably not your problem and the diet should stop.
2. **Reintroduce.** Keeping the rest of your diet low in FODMAPs, test one group at a time over a few days each, increasing the portion and noting symptoms. This usually takes a couple of months.
3. **Personalize.** Bring back everything you tolerated and limit only the foods, and the amounts, that caused trouble. The goal is the most varied diet your gut will accept.

> [!TIP] Work with a dietitian if you can
> Gastroenterology guidelines in the US and UK say the diet should be supervised by a trained dietitian.[^6][^9] If that is not possible, the Monash team publishes tested food lists and portion sizes, which are more reliable than random lists online.[^7]

## How well does it work?

In the best-known trial, 30 people with IBS ate either a low-FODMAP diet or a typical Australian diet for three weeks each, with all food provided. About 70% felt better on the low-FODMAP diet, and overall symptom scores were roughly halved.[^10] A pooled analysis of 13 randomized trials ranked it first among dietary approaches for overall symptoms.[^11]

> [!STAT] 50 to 75%
> The approximate share of people with IBS who improve on a low-FODMAP diet in trials and clinic studies.[^8][^10]

Two caveats. Diet trials are hard to blind and most were short, so the evidence is rated low quality: the ACG makes only a conditional recommendation for a limited trial.[^3][^11] And a meaningful minority of people do not respond at all.

## Who should not do it, and common mistakes

> [!WARNING]
> The low-FODMAP diet is not suitable for people with a current or past eating disorder or those at risk of malnutrition. Expert guidance advises screening for disordered eating first.[^9]

The usual mistakes are staying in the strict phase for months, skipping reintroduction and stacking other restrictions on top. A gentler version that limits only the main offenders, such as wheat, onion, garlic, legumes and milk, is a reasonable option for many people.[^9]

## Other treatments worth knowing

- **Peppermint oil.** The ACG suggests it for overall symptoms.[^3]
- **Symptom-targeted medicines.** Options include antispasmodics, loperamide for diarrhea and laxatives for constipation, with prescription drugs for IBS-C and IBS-D if these fail.[^6]
- **Gut-brain neuromodulators.** Low-dose tricyclic antidepressants are a recommended second-line treatment. They are used for their effect on gut nerves, not on mood.[^3][^6]
- **Gut-directed psychological therapy.** IBS-specific cognitive behavioral therapy and gut-directed hypnotherapy are supported by both US and UK guidelines.[^3][^6]
- **Probiotics.** Advice differs: the ACG suggests against them, while the British Society of Gastroenterology says a trial of up to 12 weeks is reasonable, stopping if there is no benefit.[^3][^6] More in our [probiotics guide](/articles/probiotics-prebiotics-fermented-foods).

Because [stress](/articles/stress-and-your-body) and poor sleep can aggravate symptoms, looking after both is part of treatment, not an optional extra.

## Red flags

IBS does not cause bleeding, weight loss or anemia. Those need a different explanation.

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor promptly if you have blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, a lump in your abdomen, signs of anemia such as unusual tiredness and pale skin, bowel symptoms that wake you from sleep, or a family history of bowel cancer, ovarian cancer, celiac disease or inflammatory bowel disease.[^3][^5][^6][^12] A lasting change in bowel habit that starts after about age 50 also needs investigating, not a diet.[^3][^5]

## The bottom line

Get a proper diagnosis first, then start with the simple measures: regular meals, soluble fiber, exercise and less caffeine and alcohol. If symptoms persist, try the low-FODMAP diet as a structured, time-limited experiment, ideally with a dietitian, and always follow it with reintroduction. If food changes are not enough, effective medicines and gut-directed therapies exist, so keep going back to your doctor.
