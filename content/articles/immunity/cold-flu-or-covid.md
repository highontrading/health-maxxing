You wake up with a scratchy throat, a heavy head and the creeping sense that the next few days are cancelled. The question that follows used to be simple (cold or flu?) and now has a third option.

Telling them apart matters less for how you treat yourself at home, which is much the same, and more for two decisions: whether you should ask about antiviral treatment, and how careful you need to be around other people. This guide covers the clues, the home care that helps, and the warning signs.

## The patterns, and their limits

Each illness has a typical shape.

| Feature | Cold | Flu | COVID-19 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Gradual | Sudden | Varies |
| Fever | Uncommon in adults | Common | Common |
| Body aches | Slight | Common, can be severe | Common |
| Runny or stuffy nose | Common | Sometimes | Common |
| Exhaustion | Mild | Common | Common |
| Loss of taste or smell | Rare | Rare | Sometimes |

A **cold** creeps in over a day or two: sore throat, runny or blocked nose, sneezing, then a cough. Most people are better within 7 to 10 days. Adults average two to three colds a year, and children more.[^1]

**[Flu](/conditions/influenza)** arrives abruptly. Fever or chills, muscle aches, headache, a dry cough and deep tiredness are typical, and many people can name the hour it started.[^2] Most recover within a week or two, but flu can lead to pneumonia and other complications. Worldwide, it causes an estimated 3 to 5 million cases of severe illness and 290,000 to 650,000 respiratory deaths a year.[^8]

**[COVID-19](/conditions/covid-19)** is the shape-shifter. Symptoms appear 2 to 14 days after exposure and can include fever, cough, sore throat, congestion, fatigue, muscle aches, headache, shortness of breath, nausea or diarrhea, and a new loss of taste or smell.[^3] It can pass for a cold or flatten you like flu.

Other viruses, including respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), cause similar illnesses, so this is not a complete list.

> [!NOTE]
> The overlap is large. Because flu and COVID-19 share so many symptoms, the two cannot be told apart reliably without a test.[^2][^3]

## Testing: the only sure way

If the answer would change what you do, test. That applies most to people who could benefit from antiviral treatment and to anyone about to spend time with someone vulnerable.

Home antigen tests are convenient but can miss an infection in its first days. If you have symptoms and the first test is negative, test again 48 hours later.[^4] A clinic or pharmacy can test for flu and COVID-19 at the same visit, and combined home tests are sold in some countries.

## Home care that helps

For most healthy people, all three illnesses are managed the same way.

- **Rest and fluids.** Sleep as much as you need and drink enough that your urine stays pale.[^12]
- **Pain and fever relief.** Paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen ease fever, headache and aches. Use them as directed on the label, and check combination cold remedies so you do not double up on the same ingredient.[^5][^7]
- **Honey for cough.** A Cochrane review found that honey probably eases cough in children more than no treatment or placebo, and it is at least a reasonable thing for adults to try.[^6] Never give honey to a baby under 12 months because of the risk of infant botulism.[^6]
- **Soothing measures.** A hot lemon and honey drink, salt-water gargles for adults and salt-water nose sprays or drops can all make you more comfortable.[^5]

What will not help: antibiotics. They do not work against viruses, will not speed recovery and can cause side effects.[^5] See [antibiotics: when they help and when they hurt](/articles/antibiotics-when-they-help).

> [!WARNING]
> Do not give aspirin to children or teenagers with a viral illness, because of its link with Reye syndrome, a rare but serious condition. Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines are not recommended for young children; check the label and ask a pharmacist.[^7]

## Antivirals: who should act early

Antiviral medicines exist for both flu and COVID-19. They are not needed by most healthy people, but they can prevent severe illness in those at higher risk, and timing is everything.

> [!STAT] 48 hours
> Flu antivirals work best when started within about two days of the first symptoms.[^8]

For COVID-19, treatment needs to begin within five to seven days of symptoms starting, depending on the medicine.[^9]

Contact a doctor or pharmacist promptly, ideally on the first day, if you have symptoms and are in a higher-risk group. These include:[^8][^9]

- older adults, with risk rising steadily with age
- pregnant women
- people with a weakened immune system
- people with long-term conditions such as heart, lung or kidney disease or diabetes
- very young children, for flu

Eligibility rules and available medicines differ between countries, so check local guidance.

## Staying home and going back out

You are most contagious early in the illness, and with flu and COVID-19 you can pass the virus on before symptoms begin.[^8][^10]

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention now uses one rule for all common respiratory viruses. Stay home and away from others until, for at least 24 hours, your symptoms have been getting better overall and you have had no fever without fever-reducing medicine. For the next five days, take extra care: wear a mask around others, keep some distance, wash your hands and let in fresh air.[^10]

In the UK, the NHS advises trying to stay at home and avoid contact with others if you have symptoms and either a high temperature or do not feel well enough for your normal activities.[^11]

Whichever rule applies to you, be especially careful around babies, older adults and anyone with a weakened immune system.

## When to get medical help

> [!DOCTOR]
> Call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department for difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain or pressure, new confusion, being hard to wake, bluish or gray lips or face, seizures, or signs of dehydration such as passing no urine.[^2][^3] In children, also watch for fast or labored breathing, ribs pulling in with each breath and not being alert or interacting. Any fever in a baby under 3 months needs urgent assessment.[^2]

See a doctor without delay if:[^1][^5][^12]

- symptoms improve and then return or get worse
- a fever lasts more than three or four days
- cold symptoms have not improved after about 10 days
- you are pregnant, aged 65 or over, or have a long-term condition or weakened immune system and you think you have flu or COVID-19

More on reading a temperature in [fever: what it means and when to worry](/articles/fever-when-to-worry).

## Prevention

Vaccines are the most effective tool: a yearly flu vaccine and whatever COVID-19 and RSV vaccination your national program offers you. Our [adult vaccines guide](/articles/adult-vaccines-guide) explains how to check. Beyond that, the basics work: wash your hands, cover coughs and sneezes, improve ventilation, and stay home when you are ill.[^10]

## The bottom line

You usually cannot be sure which virus you have without a test, and for a healthy adult it often does not change much: rest, fluids and simple pain relief. Test early and call a doctor quickly if you are at higher risk, because antivirals only work when started promptly. And treat trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion or a relapse after improvement as reasons to seek care straight away.
