The knee is a hinge with a hard job. It carries several times your body weight on stairs, absorbs every landing, and has less built-in stability than the hip above it. It is no surprise that it complains.

Most knee pain is not a sign of serious damage, and most of it responds better to the right kind of movement than to rest or surgery. This guide covers what the location of the pain suggests, how to exercise a sore knee, what the evidence says about running and keyhole surgery, and the signs that need a doctor.

## Where it hurts is the first clue

Location and timing narrow the possibilities a long way.[^1][^2][^3]

| Where and when | Common cause | Typical clues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front, around or behind the kneecap | Patellofemoral pain ("runner's knee") | Worse on stairs, squatting and after sitting with knees bent |
| Just below the kneecap | Patellar tendon overload | Jumping and running sports |
| Inner or outer side, after a twist | Meniscus or ligament injury | Swelling, catching, a feeling of instability |
| Outer side, in runners and cyclists | Iliotibial band irritation | Comes on at a predictable point in a session |
| Both knees, gradual, age 45 and over | Osteoarthritis | Brief morning stiffness, pain with activity |
| Hot, red, suddenly very painful | Gout or infection | Needs same-day assessment |

A table like this suggests, it does not diagnose. But it helps you describe the problem, and for the two commonest causes, patellofemoral pain and [osteoarthritis](/conditions/osteoarthritis), the first-line treatment turns out to be the same.

## Manage the load, do not remove it

Joints, tendons and muscles adapt to the work asked of them, and they are irritated by sudden jumps in that work: a new sport, more hills, a doubling of weekly distance. Patellofemoral pain often follows exactly that kind of change.[^2]

Total rest feels safe but weakens the muscles that support the knee. A better approach has three parts:

1. **Scale back the aggravating activity** for a few weeks. Shorter runs, fewer stairs, shallower squats.
2. **Keep doing what is comfortable.** Cycling, swimming and walking on the flat usually are.
3. **Build back gradually** as symptoms settle.

A useful rule of thumb: mild discomfort during exercise that has settled by the next morning is acceptable. Sharp pain, new swelling, or pain that is worse the following day means you did too much.

## Strengthen the hips and thighs

The thigh muscles control the kneecap and absorb load. The hip muscles control how the thigh bone moves above the knee. Strengthening both is the best-supported treatment for the common causes of knee pain.

For patellofemoral pain, an international consensus statement recommends exercise therapy to reduce pain in the short, medium and long term, and recommends combining hip and knee exercises over knee exercises alone.[^4] For knee osteoarthritis, NICE, which writes guidance for the NHS in England, says exercise should be offered to everyone, and warns that pain may increase a little at the start before it improves.[^5]

A simple starting set, two or three times a week:

| Exercise | Main muscles | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sit-to-stand | Thighs, buttocks | Rise from a chair without using your hands, lower slowly |
| Step-up | Thighs, buttocks | Step onto a low step, straighten the knee, step down under control |
| Bridge | Buttocks, hamstrings | Lie on your back, knees bent, lift your hips |
| Side-lying leg raise | Outer hip | Lie on your side, lift the top leg with the knee straight |
| Calf raise | Calves | Rise onto your toes, lower slowly |

Aim for two or three sets of 8 to 12 repetitions, and add load or a higher step as it gets easy. The [exercise library](/exercises) shows technique, and [strength training for beginners](/articles/strength-training-for-beginners) explains how to progress.

> [!TIP] Body weight multiplies at the knee
> In a study of older adults with knee osteoarthritis, every pound of weight lost took about four pounds of load off the knee with each step.[^6] If you carry extra weight, even a modest loss adds up over thousands of steps a day.

Other tools play supporting roles. For kneecap pain, shoe inserts may reduce pain in the short term.[^4] For knee osteoarthritis, NICE recommends an anti-inflammatory gel as the first medicine to try.[^5]

## Will running wreck your knees?

This fear keeps many people from a healthy habit, and the data do not support it. A meta-analysis pooling studies of more than 100,000 people found hip or knee osteoarthritis in 3.5% of recreational runners, compared with 10.2% of sedentary non-runners and 13.3% of competitive, elite-level runners.[^7]

The usual caution applies. These were observational studies, and people with sore joints may give up running, which would flatter the runners. Still, there is no sign here that recreational running wears out healthy knees. If you run, the bigger risk is ramping up too fast: see [running injuries](/articles/running-injuries-prevention).

> [!STAT] 3.5% vs 10.2%
> The share of recreational runners and of sedentary people with hip or knee osteoarthritis in a pooled analysis of observational studies.[^7]

## Scans, tears and keyhole surgery

An MRI of a middle-aged knee will often show a torn meniscus, the cartilage pad that cushions the joint. That sounds like an explanation. Often it is not.

When researchers scanned nearly 1,000 randomly selected adults aged 50 to 90, meniscus tears were common and became more so with age, and 61% of the people with a tear had no knee pain, aching or stiffness.[^8] A tear on a scan can simply be part of an aging knee.

That finding helps explain a famous trial. Finnish surgeons randomly assigned 146 people with a degenerative meniscus tear to keyhole surgery to trim it, or to a sham operation in which nothing was trimmed. A year later, the two groups had improved equally.[^9]

In 2017 an expert panel reviewing all the trials made a strong recommendation against arthroscopic (keyhole) surgery for nearly all patients with degenerative knee disease, including those with meniscus tears.[^10] NICE likewise advises against arthroscopic washout for osteoarthritis.[^5] A sudden tear in a young person after a sports injury is a different situation, and surgery may well be the right call there.

## When it is an injury

Some knees need assessment, not exercises.

> [!DOCTOR]
> Get urgent medical help if, after an injury, your knee swells within a few hours, you felt or heard a pop, it has changed shape, or you cannot put weight on it. Also get same-day help for a knee that is hot, red and very painful, especially with a fever, which can mean infection.[^1] See a doctor if your knee locks, gives way, or has not improved after a few weeks of sensible self-care.

Emergency departments use a checklist called the Ottawa knee rule to decide who needs an X-ray after an injury. The triggers are being 55 or older, tenderness over the kneecap or the bony bump on the outer side just below the knee, being unable to bend the knee to 90 degrees, or being unable to take four steps.[^11] For first aid in the first days, see [sprains and strains](/articles/sprains-and-strains-first-aid).

## The bottom line

Work out where your knee hurts and what set it off, ease back without stopping, and start strengthening your hips and thighs two or three times a week. Keep running if you enjoy it. Be skeptical of scans and keyhole surgery for a knee that has worn gradually, and take locking, giving way, rapid swelling or an inability to bear weight to a doctor.
