"Arthritis" simply means joint inflammation, and it is used for more than a hundred different conditions. The two best known, osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis, share a name, a symptom and little else. They have different causes, behave differently and need opposite kinds of urgency.

Mixing them up matters. One is managed mainly with movement, the other with medicines that should be started within weeks. This explainer puts them side by side, covers what works for each, and adds a short word on gout.

## Side by side

| Feature | Osteoarthritis | Rheumatoid arthritis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Gradual change in the whole joint | Immune system attacks the joint lining |
| How it starts | Slowly, over years | Over weeks to months |
| Morning stiffness | Eases within 30 minutes | Lasts longer, often over an hour |
| Joints | Knees, hips, spine, finger ends, base of thumb. Often one side | Small joints of hands and feet, wrists. Usually both sides |
| The joint feels | Bony, sometimes creaky | Warm, soft and swollen |
| Whole-body symptoms | None | Fatigue, sometimes fever and weight loss |
| Main treatment | Exercise, weight loss, pain relief | Disease-modifying drugs, started early |

## Osteoarthritis: a whole-joint condition

[Osteoarthritis](/conditions/osteoarthritis) is by far the more common of the two. The World Health Organization estimates that about 528 million people were living with it in 2019, more than double the number in 1990. Roughly three quarters are over 55, and 60% are women. The knee is the joint most often affected, followed by the hip and the hand.[^1]

It used to be described as wear and tear, as if joints were tires. That picture is out of date. Osteoarthritis involves the whole joint: cartilage thins, the bone underneath changes shape, and the lining and ligaments are affected too.[^3] Age, a previous joint injury, excess weight and family history all raise the risk.[^1]

Typical symptoms are pain that is worse with activity and better with rest, stiffness after sitting still that loosens within half an hour, and sometimes a grating sensation or bony knobs on the finger joints.[^3]

It rarely needs a scan to diagnose. NICE, which writes guidance for the NHS in England, says doctors can diagnose osteoarthritis without imaging in someone aged 45 or over who has activity-related joint pain and morning stiffness lasting no more than 30 minutes.[^4]

> [!STAT] 528 million
> People worldwide living with osteoarthritis in 2019, according to the WHO.[^1]

## What helps osteoarthritis

**Exercise comes first.** Both NICE and the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) put it at the center of treatment for everyone with osteoarthritis, whatever their age or the state of the joint.[^4][^5] Strengthening the muscles around a joint offloads it, and aerobic activity helps pain and function. The ACR also strongly recommends tai chi for knee and hip osteoarthritis.[^5] Pain may rise a little in the first weeks and then tends to fall. Our [knee pain guide](/articles/knee-pain-causes-and-exercises) has specific exercises.

**Weight loss, if you carry extra.** The ACR strongly recommends it for knee and hip osteoarthritis and notes that benefits start at around a 5% loss and grow with more.[^5] See [weight loss that lasts](/articles/weight-loss-that-lasts).

**Pain relief in the right order.** For knee osteoarthritis, NICE advises starting with an anti-inflammatory gel or cream (a topical NSAID). Anti-inflammatory tablets are the next step if needed, at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time. NICE advises against routine use of paracetamol (acetaminophen) and against strong opioids.[^4] A steroid injection can give short-term relief during a bad flare.[^4][^5]

**What does not earn a recommendation.** In a US trial of 1,583 people with knee osteoarthritis, glucosamine and chondroitin, alone or combined, did not reduce pain better than placebo over 24 weeks in the group as a whole.[^6] The ACR now strongly recommends against glucosamine, and NICE says not to offer it.[^4][^5] The ACR also recommends strongly against platelet-rich plasma and stem cell injections, where the products are unstandardized and the evidence is poor.[^5]

**Surgery.** Joint replacement is very effective for the right person. NICE says to consider referral when symptoms substantially affect quality of life and other treatment has not helped enough.[^4]

## Rheumatoid arthritis: an immune attack

[Rheumatoid arthritis](/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis) is an autoimmune disease: the immune system attacks the synovium, the thin lining of the joints. The WHO estimates that 18 million people had it in 2019, about 70% of them women.[^2]

The pattern is different from osteoarthritis. It usually begins in the small joints of the hands and feet, on both sides of the body at once. Joints are warm, swollen and tender, and morning stiffness often lasts well beyond half an hour. Because it is a whole-body disease, tiredness, a raised temperature, poor appetite and weight loss are common.[^7]

There is no single test. Doctors combine the examination with blood tests for inflammation and for antibodies such as rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP, plus scans of the joints.[^7] Blood tests can be normal early on, which is one reason the pattern of symptoms matters.

**Why speed matters.** Uncontrolled inflammation can erode cartilage and bone, and that damage is permanent. It can begin early in the disease, so guidelines treat suspected rheumatoid arthritis as urgent. NICE says anyone with persistent joint swelling of unknown cause should be referred to a specialist, and urgently if the small joints of the hands or feet are affected, more than one joint is involved, or symptoms have gone on for three months or more.[^8]

**Treatment.** The mainstay is disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs, known as DMARDs, which calm the immune attack. NICE recommends starting one ideally within three months of persistent symptoms appearing, and the ACR recommends methotrexate as the usual first choice.[^8][^9] Both follow a treat-to-target approach: the dose or drug is adjusted until the disease is in remission or at low activity. If conventional DMARDs are not enough, biologic or targeted drugs are added.[^9] Painkillers and anti-inflammatories help symptoms, but they do not protect the joints.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not sit on symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis for months, or try to manage them with supplements or diet alone. Delay is the one thing that reliably makes the outcome worse.[^8]

## Gout, briefly

Gout is a third kind of arthritis with its own signature. Urate crystals form in a joint and set off sudden, severe pain, swelling and redness, classically in the big toe and often overnight. Attacks settle within a week or two.[^10]

Flares are treated with anti-inflammatories, colchicine or steroids. For people with repeated attacks, the ACR recommends long-term urate-lowering medicine, usually allopurinol, adjusted to keep blood urate below 6 mg/dL (0.36 mmol/L).[^11] Our [gout](/conditions/gout) page covers triggers and diet.

## When to see a doctor quickly

> [!DOCTOR]
> Get same-day medical help for a single joint that becomes hot, swollen and very painful, especially with a fever or feeling unwell. This can be a joint infection, which can destroy a joint within days. See a doctor within a week or two if you have swollen joints with morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes, particularly in the hands or feet. For slow-onset joint pain that limits walking, sleep or work, book a routine appointment.

## The bottom line

If your joints ache with use and loosen up quickly in the morning, think osteoarthritis and get moving: exercise, weight loss if needed, and an anti-inflammatory gel for knees. If they are swollen, warm and stiff for a long time each morning, think rheumatoid arthritis and see a doctor soon, because early treatment prevents damage that cannot be undone.
