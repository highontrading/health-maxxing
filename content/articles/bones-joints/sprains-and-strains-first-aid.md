You step off a curb wrong, your ankle rolls, and within minutes it is swelling. For most of the past half century the instructions were four letters long: RICE, for rest, ice, compression and elevation.

The acronym is still on first-aid posters, but sports medicine has moved on from parts of it. The current thinking keeps the protection and compression, is cooler on the ice, and puts far more weight on what happens after day three. This guide walks through it. For cuts, burns and other emergencies, see [first aid basics](/articles/first-aid-basics).

## Sprain or strain?

They sound alike and are treated alike, but they are different injuries.[^1]

| Feature | Sprain | Strain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is injured | A ligament, the band joining bone to bone | A muscle or a tendon, which joins muscle to bone |
| Common sites | Ankle, wrist, knee, thumb | Hamstring, calf, lower back, groin |
| Typical cause | A joint twisted or forced past its range | A muscle overstretched or overloaded |

Both are graded by severity. In a grade 1 injury the tissue is stretched with tiny tears, and there is mild pain and swelling. Grade 2 is a partial tear, with more swelling, bruising and some loss of function. Grade 3 is a complete tear: the joint may be unstable, and you usually cannot use the limb normally.[^2]

## From RICE to PEACE and LOVE

RICE dates from the late 1970s. In 2012 a group of sports medicine researchers proposed replacing "rest" with "optimal loading", because too much rest was clearly slowing people down. They called their version POLICE.[^3]

In 2020 two Canadian physiotherapists went further in the British Journal of Sports Medicine with a two-part acronym: PEACE for the first days and LOVE for the weeks after.[^4]

| Letter | Stands for | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P | Protect | Unload and limit movement for 1 to 3 days. Let pain guide you |
| E | Elevate | Raise the limb above heart level |
| A | Avoid anti-inflammatories | They may interfere with healing. Ice is questioned too |
| C | Compress | Use an elastic bandage or tape to limit swelling |
| E | Educate | An active recovery beats passive treatments |
| L | Load | Return to normal activity as symptoms allow |
| O | Optimism | Confident, positive expectations are linked with better recovery |
| V | Vascularization | Start pain-free cardio a few days after injury |
| E | Exercise | Restore mobility, strength and balance |

It is worth being clear about what this is. PEACE and LOVE is an editorial, a considered expert summary, not a trial that tested the whole package against RICE. Its strongest elements, early loading and exercise, have good trial support. Its most debated ones are about ice and pills.

## The first three days

1. **Stop and protect it.** Do not play on. Limit movement and weight on the injury for one to three days, using pain as your guide. Crutches or a brace can help with a bad ankle or knee. For knee injuries in particular, see our [knee pain guide](/articles/knee-pain-causes-and-exercises).
2. **Compress.** Wrap the area with an elastic bandage, snug but not tight enough to cause numbness, tingling or color change.
3. **Elevate.** Prop the limb above the level of your heart when you can.
4. **Manage pain sensibly.** See the next two sections.
5. **Skip heat, alcohol and massage** for the first couple of days. The NHS advises avoiding them because they can increase swelling.[^5]
6. **Do not baby it for long.** After the first day or two, begin gentle movement within what pain allows.

### What about ice?

Ice numbs pain, and that is a perfectly good reason to use it. The NHS suggests an ice pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every two to three hours.[^5]

The claim that ice speeds healing is another matter. A review of the trials on RICE for ankle sprains concluded there was not enough evidence to say whether any of its components works.[^6] The PEACE and LOVE authors go a step further and suggest ice might slow healing by damping down inflammation, which is part of repair. That concern is theoretical: it has not been shown in trials of injured people.[^4]

> [!NOTE]
> A fair reading: ice for comfort is fine. Icing for hours in the belief that it heals the injury is not supported.

### What about anti-inflammatory tablets?

Here official advice differs. The NHS recommends paracetamol (acetaminophen) first and advises avoiding ibuprofen tablets for the first 48 hours, because they may slow healing.[^5] A clinical guideline on ankle sprains says anti-inflammatories can be used for pain and swelling, with the caution that they may delay natural healing.[^8]

On pain relief itself, a Cochrane review found no important difference between anti-inflammatory tablets and paracetamol for soft tissue injuries.[^7] So if you want to avoid the question, paracetamol is a reasonable first choice. Use any painkiller as directed on the label.

## Do you need an X-ray?

Swelling and bruising do not tell you whether a bone is broken. Emergency clinicians use the Ottawa ankle rules, which say an ankle or foot X-ray is needed only if there is pain near the ankle bones or in the midfoot plus any one of these:[^9]

- tenderness when pressing on the back edge or tip of the bony bump on either side of the ankle
- tenderness at the base of the fifth metatarsal, the bone on the outer edge of the midfoot, or over the navicular, on the inner side of the midfoot
- being unable to take four steps both immediately after the injury and when examined.

A systematic review of 27 studies found the rules catch nearly every fracture, and estimated that using them could cut unnecessary X-rays by 30% to 40%.[^9]

> [!DOCTOR]
> Go to an emergency department if you heard a crack, the limb or joint looks deformed or points the wrong way, the area is numb, cold or discolored, or you cannot put any weight on it. Get it checked within a day or two if pain and swelling are getting worse instead of better, if the joint feels unstable, or if you felt a sudden snap at the back of your heel and cannot rise onto your toes.[^5]

## After day three: load it

This is where recovery is won. Tissue heals stronger when it is used, and long immobilization leaves joints stiff and muscles weak.

In a trial of 101 people with mild to moderate ankle sprains, those who began therapeutic exercises in the first week had better ankle function at one and two weeks than those given standard rest-and-ice advice.[^10] The ankle sprain guideline recommends starting exercise as soon as possible, and prefers a brace or tape that allows movement over rigid immobilization.[^8]

A rough sequence for a sprained ankle:

1. **Days 2 to 7.** Ankle circles and writing the alphabet with your foot. Walk short distances as pain allows.
2. **Weeks 1 to 3.** Calf raises, resistance band work in all directions, standing on one leg.
3. **Weeks 3 to 8.** Jogging in a straight line, then hopping, cutting and sport-specific drills.

Let pain set the pace: mild discomfort that settles afterward is fine, while sharp pain or new swelling means step back. Most sprains and strains feel better after about two weeks, but the NHS advises holding off strenuous exercise such as running for up to eight weeks, and severe injuries can take months.[^5] If running is your sport, the return rules in [running injuries](/articles/running-injuries-prevention) apply here too.

## Preventing the next one

A sprained ankle is the best predictor of another. The ankle sprain guideline notes that up to 40% of people go on to have lasting instability.[^8] Rehabilitation is what lowers that risk.

- **Balance training.** In a trial of 522 athletes who had recently sprained an ankle, an eight-week home balance program cut repeat sprains over the following year from 33% to 22%.[^11]
- **Bracing or taping** for sport is recommended for people with a previous sprain.[^8]
- **Strength for strains.** For hamstring strains, programs that include the Nordic hamstring exercise roughly halved hamstring injuries across studies of more than 8,000 athletes.[^12]

> [!TIP] The toothbrush drill
> Stand on the injured leg while you brush your teeth, twice a day. When that is easy, close your eyes. It is two minutes of balance training you will not forget to do.

## The bottom line

Protect a fresh sprain or strain for a day or two, compress and elevate it, and use ice or paracetamol if you need them for pain. Check the X-ray rules if it is an ankle. Then get moving: gentle motion first, strength and balance next, and sport last. The rehab you do in weeks two to eight is what keeps it from happening again.
