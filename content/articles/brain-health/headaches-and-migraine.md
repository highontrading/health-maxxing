Almost everyone gets headaches, and headache disorders are among the most common conditions of the nervous system worldwide.[^1] Yet most people manage them with guesswork: whatever tablets are in the cupboard, taken late, and a vague theory about chocolate.

A little structure helps a lot. This guide covers the three headache types worth knowing, what a migraine attack involves, what the evidence says about triggers, how treatment is stepped up, and the warning signs that mean a headache should not wait.

## Three common types

Doctors diagnose these "primary" headaches, meaning headaches not caused by another illness, from the pattern of symptoms. No scan or blood test is needed when the pattern is typical.[^3] The definitions come from the International Classification of Headache Disorders.[^2]

| | Tension-type | Migraine | Cluster |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pain | Pressing or tight, both sides, mild to moderate | Throbbing, often one side, moderate to severe | Severe, stabbing, around or behind one eye |
| Lasts | 30 minutes to several days | 4 to 72 hours | 15 minutes to 3 hours, up to several times a day |
| Comes with | Little else | Nausea, sensitivity to light and sound, worse with movement | Red or watering eye, blocked nostril, restlessness |

Tension-type headache is the most common and the least disabling. Migraine affects about 1 in 5 women and 1 in 15 men.[^4] Cluster headache is rare but severe, more common in men, and comes in bouts lasting weeks or months, often at the same time each day.[^5]

Mixed pictures are common, and migraine is sometimes mistaken for a sinus or stress headache. A diary of when headaches happen, how long they last and what you took makes diagnosis far easier.[^3]

## What a migraine attack looks like

Migraine is a brain condition, not just a bad headache, and an attack can move through up to four phases.[^11]

1. **Prodrome.** Hours to a day beforehand: yawning, food cravings, mood changes, a stiff neck or tiredness.
2. **Aura.** About one in three people get temporary neurological symptoms, most often visual, such as zigzag lines, flashing lights or blind spots. Aura develops over about five minutes and lasts up to an hour.[^4]
3. **Headache.** Throbbing pain with nausea and sensitivity to light, sound and movement, lasting 4 to 72 hours if untreated.[^2]
4. **Postdrome.** A washed-out "migraine hangover" that can last a day or so.

## Triggers: real, but overrated

Stress, missed meals, poor sleep, alcohol, hormonal changes around periods and bright light are all commonly reported triggers.[^4] The evidence that they reliably cause attacks is weaker than most people assume.

In one experiment, researchers took 27 people who were sure that bright light or hard exercise triggered their migraine with aura and exposed them to exactly that. Only three had an attack with aura, and three more had a migraine without one.[^6] One explanation is that the prodrome fools us. A craving for chocolate or a sensitivity to light may be the first symptom of an attack that has already begun, not its cause.

The practical lesson is to avoid building a long list of forbidden foods and activities. Regular sleep, regular meals, enough fluid, exercise and managing stress are sensible foundations, and a diary will show whether any trigger is consistent for you.[^4] [Caffeine](/articles/caffeine-benefits-and-limits) cuts both ways: it can ease an attack, while heavy use and withdrawal can both bring headaches on.

## Treating an attack

The principle is to treat early, with an adequate dose, and to step up if a treatment fails.

**Tension-type headache.** Paracetamol (acetaminophen), aspirin or an anti-inflammatory such as ibuprofen, taken as directed on the label. Opioids are not recommended.[^3]

**Migraine.** UK guidance recommends a triptan combined with an anti-inflammatory or paracetamol, or any of these alone if you prefer, plus an anti-sickness medicine when needed. It advises against opioids.[^3] Triptans are prescription medicines in most countries and are not suitable for some people with heart or circulation problems.[^8]

Two newer classes can help when triptans fail or cannot be used: gepants, which block a pain-signaling molecule called CGRP, and lasmiditan.[^8] Newer is not stronger, though. A 2024 network meta-analysis of 137 trials found that several triptans outperformed the newer drugs, whose effect was closer to that of ordinary painkillers.[^7]

> [!WARNING] Painkillers can cause headaches
> Medication-overuse headache can develop when people with migraine or tension-type headache take simple painkillers on 15 or more days a month, or triptans, opioids or combination painkillers on 10 or more days a month, for more than three months.[^2] As a rule of thumb, keep acute treatment to about two days a week, and see a doctor if you need more.

## Preventing attacks

If migraine attacks are frequent or badly disrupt your life, ask about prevention. The American Headache Society suggests considering it from about four headache days a month, or fewer if attacks are disabling.[^8]

- **Established medicines.** These include certain blood pressure, epilepsy and antidepressant drugs used at doses chosen for migraine. They take weeks to work, and some are unsuitable in pregnancy.[^3]
- **CGRP-targeting drugs.** Monthly or quarterly antibody injections and daily gepant tablets were designed specifically for migraine. In 2024 the American Headache Society said they can be considered a first-line preventive option, citing their effectiveness and tolerability.[^9] Cost and access vary widely between countries.
- **Other options.** Botulinum toxin injections are used for chronic migraine, meaning headaches on 15 or more days a month. NICE also notes that riboflavin (400 mg a day) may help some people, and recommends a course of acupuncture if standard preventives fail.[^3]

No preventive stops every attack. A realistic aim is fewer, shorter and milder ones.

> [!NOTE]
> If you have migraine with aura, tell whoever prescribes your contraception. Combined hormonal contraceptives are not routinely recommended for people with migraine with aura, because of a small added risk of stroke.[^3]

## Cluster headache needs its own plan

Ordinary painkillers do little for cluster headache because attacks peak too fast. The treatments that work are high-flow oxygen through a mask and triptans given by injection or nasal spray, with preventive medicine during a bout.[^5] If your headaches fit the cluster column in the table, ask for a referral.

## When a headache is a warning sign

The great majority of headaches are not dangerous, but the exceptions matter. Doctors use checklists of red flags to find them.[^10]

> [!DOCTOR]
> Call your local emergency number for a sudden, severe headache that peaks within about a minute; a headache with fever and a stiff neck, a rash, confusion or a seizure; new weakness, numbness, speech trouble or loss of vision; or a headache after a head injury. See a doctor promptly for a new type of headache after age 50, especially with scalp tenderness or jaw pain when chewing; headaches that are getting steadily worse; headaches that are worse lying down or with coughing or straining; and any new headache in pregnancy or in someone with cancer or a weakened immune system.[^3][^10]

## The bottom line

Work out which type of headache you have, treat attacks early, and keep acute medicines to about two days a week. If migraine is frequent, effective prevention exists and is worth asking about. Learn the red flags once, so that you can treat ordinary headaches with confidence and unusual ones with urgency.
