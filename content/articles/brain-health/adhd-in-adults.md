For a long time, ADHD was thought of as something restless schoolboys grew out of. Neither half of that picture has survived. It affects girls and women too, and for many people it continues into adult life, long after anyone is watching them fidget in a classroom.

Awareness has risen sharply, helped by social media, and so have assessments. That is good news for people who struggled for years without an explanation, and it also brings confusion about what ADHD is and is not. This guide covers how it presents in adults, how diagnosis works, what can look like ADHD, and what helps.

## What ADHD is

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder is a neurodevelopmental condition: a difference in how the brain develops and regulates attention, activity and impulses. It is defined by a persistent pattern of inattention, hyperactivity-impulsivity or both that interferes with functioning.[^1] It runs strongly in families, and an international consensus of researchers concluded that it is a valid condition with measurable effects on education, work, relationships, accidents and health.[^5]

It is not laziness, low intelligence or a failure of character. Nor is it the ordinary distraction everyone feels when a task is dull and a phone is within reach. The difference lies in how persistent, widespread and impairing the difficulties are.

## How it shows up in adults

Hyperactivity often fades or turns inward with age, while problems with attention and organization tend to persist.[^1] Adults with ADHD commonly describe:

- Difficulty sustaining attention on tasks that are not immediately interesting, alongside intense focus on those that are.
- Chronic disorganization, lateness and trouble estimating how long things take.
- Starting many things and finishing few, with a lot of procrastination.
- Forgetting appointments, losing keys, missing bills.
- Inner restlessness, talking over people, impatience when waiting in line or in traffic.
- Impulsive decisions about spending, jobs or relationships.
- Quick frustration and strong emotional reactions.

### Why women are diagnosed late

Girls and women with ADHD are more likely to have mainly inattentive symptoms, which disrupt a classroom less than hyperactivity does, and they are more likely to compensate by working harder to appear organized. Many are first treated for anxiety or depression, with the underlying ADHD recognized only years later.[^6]

## How common is it?

A meta-analysis of studies from around the world estimated that about 2.6% of adults have ADHD that has persisted from childhood. A broader definition, counting adults with enough current symptoms regardless of when they began, gave a figure of nearly 7%.[^3]

Diagnosis rates are rising toward those numbers. In a 2023 national survey, about 6% of US adults, roughly 15.5 million people, reported a current ADHD diagnosis, and about half of them had been diagnosed in adulthood.[^4]

> The share of US adults with a current ADHD diagnosis who were first diagnosed at 18 or older.[^4]

## How diagnosis works

There is no blood test, brain scan or computer task that can diagnose ADHD. Diagnosis rests on a thorough clinical assessment against agreed criteria:[^1][^2]

- **Enough symptoms.** For people aged 17 and over, at least five symptoms of inattention, hyperactivity-impulsivity or both, lasting six months or more.
- **Childhood onset.** Several symptoms present before age 12. School reports and a parent's or sibling's memories can help.
- **More than one setting.** For example, at work and at home.
- **Clear impairment.** The symptoms interfere with work, study, relationships or daily life.
- **No better explanation.** Other conditions have been considered.

UK guidance says diagnosis should be made only by a specialist with training in ADHD, after a full clinical and psychosocial assessment, and never from a rating scale alone.[^2] Our [ADHD](/conditions/adhd) page has a short summary.

> [!NOTE] What social media can and cannot tell you
> Videos about ADHD have helped many adults recognize themselves and seek help. They are also unreliable: when researchers rated the 100 most popular TikTok videos on ADHD, they classed 52% as misleading and only about a fifth as useful.[^10] Recognizing yourself in a list of traits is a reason to ask for an assessment. It is not a diagnosis, because the same traits have many causes.

## What can look like ADHD

Trouble concentrating is one of the least specific symptoms in medicine. Stress, other mental health conditions and physical illnesses can all produce it,[^1] and several commonly occur together with ADHD, so a good assessment looks for both.

| Condition | Clue that points toward it |
| --- | --- |
| Anxiety or depression | Difficulties began with a change in mood, and vary with it |
| Sleep loss or sleep apnea | Daytime sleepiness, snoring, unrefreshing sleep |
| Thyroid problems, anemia, other illness | Tiredness and physical symptoms, with a recent onset |
| Alcohol, cannabis or other substances | Symptoms track with use |
| Bipolar disorder | Distinct episodes of elevated mood and reduced need for sleep |
| Burnout or overload | A clear starting point linked to circumstances |

The key question is timing. ADHD is lifelong and fairly consistent. Problems that first appeared in adulthood are more likely to have another cause. Our guides to [anxiety](/articles/understanding-anxiety) and [depression](/articles/depression-signs-and-treatment) describe two of the commonest.

## What helps

### Medication

Medicines have the strongest evidence for the core symptoms. NICE recommends offering medication to adults whose symptoms still cause significant impairment after practical adjustments have been tried. Stimulants, in the form of lisdexamfetamine or methylphenidate, are first choice, with the non-stimulant atomoxetine as an alternative.[^2]

A network meta-analysis of 133 trials found that these medicines reduced symptoms more than placebo over about 12 weeks, with amphetamine-type stimulants most effective in adults.[^7] A 2025 analysis focused on adults reached a similar conclusion: stimulants and atomoxetine were the only treatments shown to reduce core symptoms on both patients' and clinicians' ratings in the short term. It also stressed how little long-term evidence exists.[^8]

These are prescription medicines that need monitoring. Blood pressure, heart rate and weight are checked before and during treatment,[^2] and stimulants are controlled drugs that should never be shared or taken without a prescription.

### Psychological approaches

Cognitive behavioral therapy adapted for ADHD teaches skills for planning, organizing and managing distraction and unhelpful thinking. In a randomized trial of adults who still had symptoms despite medication, those who received it improved more than those given relaxation training and education.[^9] NICE recommends this kind of structured support for adults who prefer not to take medication, cannot tolerate it, or still have difficulties while on it.[^2] ADHD coaching is popular and may help with practical systems, but it has been studied far less.

### Routines and surroundings

Many strategies work by moving the burden from memory and willpower to the environment:

- Put everything in one calendar with alarms, and one task list.
- Break projects into small, concrete next steps with their own deadlines.
- Work in short timed blocks, with the phone in another room.
- Keep essentials such as keys and wallet in one fixed place.
- Ask about adjustments at work or college, such as written instructions or a quieter space.

Our guide to [building habits that stick](/articles/how-to-build-habits-that-stick) explains the principles. Regular sleep and physical activity help most people's attention and mood, and are worth protecting.

## Strengths, honestly

Many adults with ADHD describe real strengths, including energy, creativity, humor and the capacity to become absorbed in what interests them. Research on these is mostly small and descriptive, and it should not be used to minimize the difficulties. But a diagnosis is often a relief precisely because it replaces "what is wrong with me?" with an explanation and a plan.

> [!DOCTOR]
> Speak to a doctor if difficulties with attention, organization or impulsivity have been present since childhood and are harming your work, studies, relationships or finances. Ask for an assessment for ADHD and for other possible causes. Our guide on [how to talk to your doctor](/articles/how-to-talk-to-your-doctor) can help you prepare. If you are feeling hopeless or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency services or a crisis line straight away. Find A Helpline lists services by country.[^11]

## The bottom line

Adult ADHD is common, long-standing by definition, and treatable. If the description fits your whole life and not just a hard year, ask for a proper assessment, and expect it to look for other explanations too. Treatment that combines the right medicine, skills-based therapy and a supportive environment helps most people function better.
