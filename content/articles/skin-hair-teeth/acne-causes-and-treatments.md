Acne is so common that it barely counts as a misfortune. The NHS estimates that about 95% of people aged 11 to 30 are affected to some degree.[^1] That does not make it trivial. It can hurt, it can scar, and it has a way of arriving at exactly the age when people feel most looked at.

The good news is that acne is one of the better-understood skin conditions, and the treatments are tested and specific. This guide explains what is going on inside a pimple, what you can buy, what a doctor can add, and how long any of it takes.

## Four things have to go wrong

A pimple starts in a hair follicle, the tiny opening you know as a pore. Four linked processes turn it into a spot:[^2]

1. **Too much oil.** Hormones called androgens, which surge at puberty, make the oil glands produce more sebum.
2. **A plug.** Dead skin cells stick together instead of shedding and block the follicle.
3. **Bacteria.** A bacterium that normally lives harmlessly on skin multiplies in the oily plug.
4. **Inflammation.** The immune system reacts, producing redness, swelling and pus.

A plugged follicle that stays closed is a whitehead. One that opens is a blackhead, and its color comes from air reacting with the plug, not from dirt. Inflamed follicles become red bumps and pus-filled spots. The deep, painful lumps called nodules and cysts are the ones most likely to scar.[^2]

Notice what is not on the list: poor hygiene. The NHS counts the idea that acne comes from dirty skin among the common myths, and washing more than twice a day can make things worse by irritating the skin.[^1] Things that do contribute include family history, hormonal changes around periods and pregnancy, certain medicines such as steroids and lithium, greasy cosmetics, and pressure or friction from helmets and straps.[^1][^2]

## What you can buy without a prescription

The 2024 guidelines from the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) graded each treatment by the strength of the evidence. The over-the-counter options, in rough order of support:[^3]

| Treatment | What it does | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benzoyl peroxide | Kills acne bacteria | Strongly recommended. Bleaches towels and pillowcases. |
| Adapalene (a retinoid) | Keeps pores from plugging | Strongly recommended as a class. Not for use in pregnancy. |
| Azelaic acid | Calms inflammation, fades marks | Conditional recommendation. Usually gentle. |
| Salicylic acid | Loosens plugs | Conditional recommendation, thinner evidence. |

Adapalene 0.1% gel is sold over the counter in the US. In many other countries, including the UK, it needs a prescription.

Three habits make these work better. First, combine products that act in different ways, such as benzoyl peroxide plus a retinoid, which the AAD lists as good practice.[^3] Second, treat the whole acne-prone area, not just today's spots, because the aim is to prevent the next ones.[^5] Third, introduce one product at a time, a few nights a week at first, since dryness and stinging are the main reasons people give up. Use them as directed on the label, and see our [simple skincare routine](/articles/simple-skincare-routine) for how to fit them around a cleanser and moisturizer.

> [!TIP] Give it 12 weeks
> Most treatments need 6 to 12 weeks to show their effect. NICE, which writes guidance for the NHS in England, builds its recommendations around 12-week courses followed by a review.[^4] Take a photo on day one. Slow progress is hard to see in the mirror.

## What a doctor can add

If over-the-counter treatment has not done enough, the prescription options step up in order.

**Combination gels and creams.** These pair two ingredients, such as a retinoid with benzoyl peroxide, or benzoyl peroxide with the antibiotic clindamycin. NICE recommends a fixed combination as a first-line treatment for acne of any severity.[^4]

**Antibiotics.** Oral antibiotics such as doxycycline reduce bacteria and inflammation in moderate to severe acne. Two rules apply: they should be used for a limited time, and never on their own. Pairing them with benzoyl peroxide lowers the chance that bacteria become resistant.[^3] NICE advises reviewing treatment at 12 weeks and continuing antibiotics beyond six months only in exceptional circumstances.[^4]

**Hormonal therapy.** For women, combined oral contraceptive pills and spironolactone can reduce the hormonal drive behind acne. Both carry a conditional recommendation from the AAD and take a few months to work.[^3]

**Isotretinoin.** This oral retinoid is the most effective acne treatment available. The AAD strongly recommends it for acne that is severe, is causing scarring or psychological distress, or has not responded to standard treatment.[^3] A course usually lasts several months.[^6] It also demands respect: isotretinoin causes severe birth defects, so it is prescribed under strict pregnancy-prevention rules, with blood tests to monitor the liver and blood fats, and with attention to mood during treatment.[^3][^6]

> [!WARNING]
> Isotretinoin must never be taken during pregnancy, and topical retinoids, oral tetracycline antibiotics and hormonal therapy should be avoided too. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists considers over-the-counter benzoyl peroxide, azelaic acid, topical salicylic acid and glycolic acid acceptable in pregnancy.[^7]

## Does diet matter?

Less than the internet suggests, and possibly more than doctors used to think. The honest summary is that the evidence is thin.

**Glycemic load.** In a randomized trial of 43 young men with acne, those assigned to a low-glycemic-load diet for 12 weeks had a larger drop in spots than the control group. They also lost weight, which makes it hard to say what caused the improvement.[^8] Glycemic load is a measure of how much a food raises blood sugar, explained in [carbs explained](/articles/carbs-explained).

**Dairy.** A meta-analysis pooling observational studies of more than 78,000 children and young adults found that those who consumed dairy, particularly milk, were modestly more likely to have acne. Observational studies cannot prove cause, and these varied a great deal in quality and method.[^9]

The AAD guidelines conclude that there is not enough evidence to recommend any specific diet for acne.[^3] If you notice a consistent personal trigger, cutting back on it is reasonable. Cutting out whole food groups is not.

## Leave it alone, and treat marks early

Squeezing a spot can push its contents deeper, which means more inflammation and a higher chance of infection and scarring.[^5]

Acne leaves two kinds of reminder. Flat red or brown marks are discoloration, more common and longer-lasting in darker skin tones, and they usually fade over months. Daily [sunscreen](/articles/sunscreen-guide) helps. Pits and raised scars are permanent changes in the skin's structure and need procedures from a dermatologist to improve. The risk of scarring rises the longer inflamed acne goes untreated, which is the strongest argument for treating early.[^4]

## Adult acne

Acne is not only a teenage condition. It can persist into, or first appear in, adult life, and adult acne is more common in women.[^1][^5] Flares before a period are typical. Acne that arrives with irregular periods or excess facial or body hair can be a sign of a hormonal condition such as [polycystic ovary syndrome](/conditions/pcos), and is worth raising with a doctor.[^1]

It is also worth checking what touches your skin. Heavy hair products, oily makeup and some medicines can all trigger breakouts, so look for products labeled non-comedogenic.[^2]

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor if you have deep, painful lumps, if acne is leaving scars or dark marks, if it has not improved after about 12 weeks of over-the-counter treatment, or if it is affecting your mood or confidence. Sudden, severe acne with fever or joint pain needs same-day medical attention.

## The bottom line

Start with benzoyl peroxide and, if you can get it, adapalene, applied to the whole affected area, and give them 12 weeks. If that is not enough, or if you have deep lumps or scars, see a doctor: the prescription options are effective and the earlier they start, the less scarring there is to fix later. Wash gently, keep your hands off, and do not expect a diet to do the work of a treatment.
