Hair in the shower drain is not, by itself, a problem. A healthy scalp sheds between 50 and 100 hairs every day, and most of us never notice.[^1][^2] Trouble starts when the amount jumps, the part widens, or a smooth bare patch appears where there was hair last month.

Those three patterns have different causes and very different outlooks. This guide explains how to tell them apart, which tests are worth having, what treatment can do, and how long any regrowth takes.

## How hair grows, rests and falls out

Every follicle on your scalp runs its own cycle. A hair grows for several years, pauses, rests for roughly three months and then falls out as a new hair pushes up behind it. At any moment the large majority of your hairs are growing and a small share are resting, which is why you lose a few dozen a day and not all of them at once.[^3]

That cycle explains the two basic problems, which dermatologists treat as separate things:[^1]

- **Shedding.** A shock pushes many hairs into the resting phase together, and they fall out together a few months later. The follicles are fine.
- **Hair loss.** Something stops hair from growing: follicles shrink, are attacked by the immune system, or are damaged.

> [!STAT] 50 to 100
> The number of hairs a healthy scalp sheds every day.[^1]

The pattern on your head is the best first clue:

| What you notice | Likely cause | Usual outlook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Handfuls of hair from all over, months after illness, birth or stress | Telogen effluvium | Regrows on its own |
| Slow thinning on top, widening part | Pattern hair loss | Progressive, treatable |
| Smooth round bald patches | Alopecia areata | Unpredictable, treatable |
| Thinning at the hairline or temples | Traction from tight styles | Reversible if caught early |
| Bare patch with redness, scaling or pain | Scalp disease or scarring | Needs prompt assessment |

## Sudden shedding after a shock

Excessive shedding has a medical name, telogen effluvium, and a recognizable story. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) lists the usual triggers: an illness, especially one with a high fever, childbirth, an operation, a period of severe stress, losing a lot of weight, or stopping birth control pills.[^1] Iron deficiency, thyroid disease, poor nutrition and some medicines can do the same.[^2][^3][^10]

The timing fools people. Shedding typically starts two to three months after the event, by which point the cause may be forgotten.[^3] After childbirth it tends to peak at around four months.[^1]

The reassuring part is that it reverses. As the body readjusts, shedding slows, and the AAD says hair tends to regain its normal fullness within six to nine months.[^1] No product has been shown to speed this up. What helps is addressing the trigger: recovering, eating enough, and treating a deficiency or thyroid problem if one is found.

## Gradual thinning: pattern hair loss

Pattern hair loss is inherited and hormonal, and it is the most common cause of hair loss in both sexes. In women it rarely produces a bald spot. Instead the part slowly widens and the hair on top thins, while the front hairline usually stays put.[^4] In men it follows a different course, covered in [male pattern hair loss](/articles/male-pattern-hair-loss).

It does not reverse by itself, but treatment can slow it and thicken what remains:

- **Minoxidil**, applied to the scalp, is sold without a prescription and is the main medicine approved for female pattern hair loss.[^2][^4] Use it as directed on the label.
- **Prescription options** that dermatologists sometimes add include tablets that block the effect of androgens. Finasteride, widely used in men, is not recommended for women.[^2]
- **Procedures** such as hair transplants suit some people once the loss has stabilized.[^2]

> [!NOTE]
> Minoxidil tests your patience. Some extra shedding in the first weeks is common, it can take 6 to 12 months to judge the result, and the benefit lasts only as long as you keep using it.[^2][^4]

## Round bald patches: alopecia areata

In alopecia areata the immune system attacks hair follicles. It usually shows up as one or more smooth, coin-sized bald patches, and it can affect the eyebrows, lashes and beard. Hair often regrows without treatment, but the condition is unpredictable and can return.[^5]

For limited patches, doctors mainly use corticosteroids, injected or applied to the skin.[^5] For severe disease there has been real progress. Since 2022 the US Food and Drug Administration has approved medicines called JAK inhibitors for severe alopecia areata, starting with baricitinib.[^5] In two large trials in adults who had lost at least half their scalp hair, more than a third of those on the higher dose regained at least 80% scalp coverage by 36 weeks, compared with about one in twenty on placebo.[^6]

These are powerful immune-modifying drugs with serious potential side effects, and hair may fall out again if they are stopped, so the decision belongs with a dermatologist.

## Loss at the hairline: traction

Hairstyles that pull, such as tight ponytails, buns, braids, cornrows, weaves and extensions, can damage follicles over time. Early on, the hair regrows if the pulling stops. After years, the loss can become permanent.[^4]

> [!TIP] A hairstyle should not hurt
> If a style causes pain, bumps or broken hairs around the hairline, it is too tight. Loosen it, vary your styles, and give your scalp breaks between braids or extensions.

## Tests worth having

There is no single hair loss test. For diffuse shedding, doctors commonly check a full blood count, iron stores (ferritin) and thyroid function, and add hormone tests if there are signs such as irregular periods or excess body hair.[^2][^7] See [understanding blood tests](/articles/understanding-blood-tests) for what those numbers mean.

The link between low iron and hair loss is less tidy than it sounds. A review in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology concluded that the evidence was not strong enough to recommend iron screening for everyone with hair loss, although many dermatologists do test and treat low levels.[^7] The practical point: find out before you supplement, because iron you do not need can do harm.

## Supplements: test first

Hair vitamins are a large market resting on a small evidence base. A review of vitamins and minerals in hair loss found that supplements help when a true deficiency is corrected, and found little support for them otherwise. It also noted that too much of some nutrients, including vitamin A and selenium, can itself cause hair loss.[^8]

Biotin is the standard example. Deficiency is rare, and there are no good trials showing benefit in people with normal levels. High doses can interfere with laboratory tests, a problem the FDA has warned about.[^9] Our [beauty supplements evidence check](/articles/collagen-biotin-beauty-supplements) goes through the details.

## How long regrowth takes

Hair grows roughly 1 cm (a little under half an inch) a month, so every timeline here is slow. After a shedding episode, expect short new hairs along the part and hairline within a few months and fuller hair by six to nine months.[^1] With minoxidil, think in terms of a year.[^4] Photographs taken in the same light every month or two are more reliable than the mirror.

> [!DOCTOR]
> See a doctor if you have bald patches, sudden loss or hair coming out in clumps, or a scalp that is itchy, burning, red or scaly.[^2] Patches with scarring can become permanent, so do not wait. Also get checked if shedding goes on beyond six months, or comes with tiredness, weight change, feeling cold or irregular periods, which can point to thyroid disease or [iron deficiency](/conditions/iron-deficiency-anemia).

## The bottom line

Work out which pattern you have: all-over shedding after a trigger, slow thinning on top, or patches. The first usually fixes itself within months, the second responds to consistent treatment, and the third deserves a dermatologist. Get a blood test before buying supplements, and give anything you try at least six months before you judge it.
