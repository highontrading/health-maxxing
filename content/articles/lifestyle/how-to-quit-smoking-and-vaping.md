Most people who smoke would like to stop, and most have already tried. If that is you, the problem is not your character. Nicotine is addictive, cigarettes deliver it efficiently, and going it alone is the hardest way to quit.

The encouraging part is that quitting has been studied in hundreds of trials, so we know which tools raise your odds and by roughly how much. This guide ranks them, then covers the practical side: cravings, slips, and how to get off vapes as well.

## Why it is worth it, whatever your age

Tobacco kills more than 7 million people a year, and up to half of the people who use it and do not quit.[^1] The damage starts to reverse quickly once you stop:[^2]

| Time since last cigarette | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 20 minutes | Heart rate and blood pressure drop |
| 12 hours | Carbon monoxide in the blood returns to normal |
| 2 to 12 weeks | Circulation and lung function improve |
| 1 to 9 months | Coughing and shortness of breath decrease |
| 1 year | Excess risk of coronary heart disease is about half that of someone who still smokes |
| 10 years | Lung cancer risk falls to about half that of someone who still smokes |
| 15 years | Risk of coronary heart disease is similar to that of someone who never smoked |

> [!STAT] 9 years
> The life expectancy gained, on average, by quitting at about age 40 compared with continuing to smoke. Quitting at about 50 gains roughly six years, and at 60 about three.[^2]

Smoking is a leading cause of [lung cancer](/conditions/lung-cancer), [COPD](/conditions/copd) and cardiovascular disease, so stopping is one of the most effective ways to lower your [heart disease risk](/articles/heart-disease-risk-factors).[^1]

## What works, ranked by evidence

Going cold turkey with no help is the most common method and the least successful. Studies of unaided attempts suggest only about 3 to 5 in 100 last 6 to 12 months.[^12] Each of the options below improves on that.

A 2023 Cochrane network meta-analysis pooled more than 300 trials of quitting aids. Three stood out with high-certainty evidence: **varenicline**, **cytisine** and **nicotine e-cigarettes**. With any of them, roughly 14 in 100 people quit for six months or more, compared with about 6 in 100 using a placebo or nothing. **Combination nicotine replacement** came close behind. A single nicotine product or bupropion helped too, at about 9 in 100.[^3]

| Option | What it is | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Varenicline | Prescription tablet that eases cravings and makes smoking less rewarding | Among the most effective. Nausea and vivid dreams are common side effects |
| Cytisine (cytisinicline) | Plant-derived tablet that works in a similar way | Strong evidence, but availability varies by country |
| Combination nicotine replacement | A patch for background nicotine plus gum, lozenge, spray or inhalator for cravings | Beats a single product. Sold without prescription in many countries |
| Nicotine e-cigarettes | Vapes used as a complete switch from cigarettes | Effective, with caveats covered below |
| Bupropion | Prescription tablet | Works, somewhat less well than the options above |

Two points matter as much as the choice of product. First, behavioral support adds to whatever medicine you use. The US Preventive Services Task Force gives its top grade to offering adults both counseling and medication, and notes that phone quitlines and text-message programs also work.[^5] Second, finish the course. Most of these treatments are designed to be used for around two to three months, not dropped as soon as the first cravings ease.[^7]

UK guidance from NICE lists varenicline, combination nicotine replacement and nicotine e-cigarettes, each alongside behavioral support, as the options most likely to succeed.[^6]

## Where e-cigarettes fit

This is where countries disagree, so here is the evidence and then the caveats.

Cochrane's regularly updated review finds high-certainty evidence that nicotine e-cigarettes help more people quit than nicotine replacement does: roughly 10 in 100 compared with 6 in 100.[^4] The NHS describes vaping as much less harmful than smoking, though not harmless, and offers it as a quitting tool.[^7]

The caveats are real. Most trials followed people for a year or less, so the long-term effects of vaping are unknown.[^4] The benefit comes from switching completely, not from vaping on top of smoking. The US task force judged the evidence insufficient to recommend e-cigarettes and points people toward the established medicines first.[^5] In pregnancy, behavioral support comes first, so speak to your midwife or doctor about what else is suitable.[^5]

> [!NOTE]
> If you do not smoke, there is no health case for vaping. This section applies only to adults using e-cigarettes to get off cigarettes.

## Build a quit plan

1. **Pick a date** within the next two weeks and tell people who will back you.
2. **Choose your aid** from the table above, and get it before the day arrives. A pharmacist, doctor or stop-smoking service can help you choose.
3. **Line up support.** Most countries have a free quitline, and many have text or app programs.
4. **Clear the decks.** Remove cigarettes, lighters and ashtrays from your home, car and bag.
5. **Map your triggers.** List the moments you always smoke, such as with coffee, after meals, while driving or when drinking, and decide in advance what you will do instead. Our guide to [building habits that stick](/articles/how-to-build-habits-that-stick) explains why this kind of if-then planning works.

## Cravings, withdrawal and slips

Withdrawal can bring irritability, restlessness, low mood, poor concentration, trouble sleeping and a bigger appetite. Symptoms usually peak in the first few days, are worst in the first week and fade over the following weeks.[^9] A single craving typically passes within a few minutes, so the skill is outlasting it: delay, move, drink some water, use your fast-acting nicotine product, or message someone.[^9]

Two worries stop many people from trying.

**"Smoking is how I cope with [stress](/articles/stress-and-your-body)."** It feels that way because each cigarette relieves the withdrawal left by the last one. In a meta-analysis of 26 studies, people who quit ended up with less anxiety, depression and stress than those who carried on, an improvement comparable in size to that seen with antidepressant treatment.[^10] These were observational comparisons, so they cannot prove cause, but they do not support the fear that quitting wrecks your mental health.

**"I will gain weight."** Many people do: on average about 4 to 5 kg (9 to 11 lb) in the first year, with wide variation.[^11] Staying active helps, and the benefit of quitting dwarfs the cost of a few kilograms.

> [!TIP] If you slip
> One cigarette does not erase your progress. Work out what triggered it, adjust the plan, and carry on with your treatment. Most people who quit for good needed more than one attempt.

## Quitting vaping

Far fewer trials have tested how to stop vaping, so the advice is less certain. A 2025 Cochrane review found low-certainty evidence that varenicline can help adults stop, and that text-message support programs can help young people.[^8]

Until better evidence arrives, the principles from smoking apply. Set a date, step down your nicotine strength or the number of times you vape each day, keep it out of reach at set times, and get behavioral support. If you vape to stay off cigarettes, the priority is not going back to smoking, so reduce gradually and only when you feel secure.[^7]

> [!DOCTOR]
> Quitting is worthwhile for almost everyone, including people who are pregnant or have heart disease. Ask a doctor or pharmacist which quitting aids suit you if you are pregnant, have a heart condition or take regular medicines. Stopping smoking changes how the body handles some drugs, so doses may need adjusting.[^6] Seek help promptly if your mood drops sharply after quitting. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line straight away.

## The bottom line

Pick a quit date, choose one of the proven aids, and add support from a quitline, an app or a counselor. Expect the first week to be the hardest and plan for your triggers. If one method does not work, try another. Most people who quit for good needed more than one attempt, and proven tools make each attempt more likely to succeed.
