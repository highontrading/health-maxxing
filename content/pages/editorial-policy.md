Health information is only useful if you can trust it. This page explains how ours is made, so you can judge that for yourself.

## Where our information comes from

We build articles from primary and authoritative sources, in this order of preference:

1. Guidelines and fact sheets from the World Health Organization and national health bodies.
2. Position statements from professional medical and scientific societies.
3. Systematic reviews, meta-analyses and major clinical trials in peer-reviewed journals.
4. A small number of established academic medical centers, used sparingly.

We do not cite blogs, forums, supplement sellers, content farms or news stories as evidence. Every article lists its sources, and claims in the text link to the source that supports them.

## How we talk about evidence

Not all evidence is equal, and we try to make the difference visible. A randomized trial can show that something causes an effect. An observational study can only show that two things go together. When we give a number, we try to say who was studied and what it was compared with. When the research is mixed or early, we say so rather than pick a side.

## How articles are written

Articles are researched and drafted with the help of AI writing tools, working from the sources listed on each page and under the direction of our editors. We use those tools because they let a small team cover a lot of ground carefully. We do not use them to invent facts, experts, quotes or studies, and we hold AI-assisted drafts to the same sourcing rules as anything else.

## Fact-checking

Every article and condition page has a fact-check status.

- **Sources verified** means each figure, threshold and link on the page has been checked against the original source.
- Pages **without that label** are written from established guidance but have not yet completed that line-by-line check. They are in the queue.

We would rather tell you which is which than imply that everything has been verified.

## Dates and updates

Each page shows when it was last updated. Health guidance changes, and we revise pages when major guidelines do. If you find something out of date, please [tell us](/contact).

## Corrections

When we get something wrong, we fix it. Small fixes are made directly. If a correction changes the meaning or the practical advice on a page, we say so on the page.

## Independence

We do not accept payment for coverage. We do not publish sponsored articles presented as editorial. If that ever changes, paid content will be clearly labelled.

## Medical advice

Nothing on Health Maxxing is personal medical advice. Our content is general information for adults and is not a substitute for a consultation with a qualified health professional. See the [medical disclaimer](/medical-disclaimer).

## Community content

Posts in the community are written by members, not by our editors, and are not fact-checked. See the [community guidelines](/community-guidelines).
